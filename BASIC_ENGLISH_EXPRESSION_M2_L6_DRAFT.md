# Module 2 · Lesson 6 — Feeling, Preference, Desire & Need

> **Draft status:** source-reviewed first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English*, p.252. The source examples are distinguished from project-created explanations and exercises. Physical touch, knowledge/awareness and the different meanings of show remain cross-linked to their own lessons. Clarity, answer coverage and actual learner time still require review.

## What this lesson teaches

The source uses several expressions for a person's inner experience and attitude. Do not treat **feel**, **like**, **approve**, **want** and **need** as interchangeable.

This lesson has three parts:

1. feeling and internal experience;
2. liking, friendship, preference and approval;
3. desire and need.

The main task is to identify the kind of relation: an experience, a personal liking, a social relationship, an approval judgment, a desire or a need.

## 1. Feeling and internal experience

The source gives three internal uses of **feel**:

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is being described |
|---|---|---|
| feel pain | have a feeling of pain | An experience of pain |
| feel pleasure | have a feeling of pleasure | An experience of pleasure |
| feel that something is so | have a feeling / idea that it is so | An impression or idea, not necessarily a firm judgment |
| feel angry | be feeling angry | An emotional state |

Keep these uses separate:

- **have a feeling of pain / pleasure** names the experience;
- **have a feeling / idea that it is so** expresses an impression or idea;
- **be feeling angry** describes a person's emotional state.

A useful comparison with C05:

- **think that something is so** → be of the opinion / take the view that;
- **feel that something is so** → have a feeling / idea that it is so.

The source places the second expression with its feeling examples. Do not automatically replace it with the C05 opinion expression.

## 2. Liking, friendship, preference and approval

The source uses different expressions depending on whether the object is a thing, a person, or something one has a taste for. It then adds a more general expression of approval.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is being described |
|---|---|---|
| like a thing | be pleased with it | A positive personal response |
| like a person | be friends with them / get on well with them | A friendly relationship |
| like good food or pictures | have a taste for them | A preference |
| like something in a more general sense | it has our approval | The thing is approved of |
| express approval of something | give our approval to it | Approval is actively given |

Keep the different relations clear:

- **be pleased with a thing** describes a positive response to it;
- **be friends with a person** expresses a friendship; **get on well with a person** expresses having a good relationship. The source gives both as ways to express liking a person, but they are not identical relationships;
- **have a taste for** food or pictures describes preference;
- **it has our approval** and **give our approval to it** express the more general approval relation.

The source gives positive approval expressions here. It does not provide a corresponding negative Basic expression in this passage, so this lesson does not invent one.

Here **have a taste for** means preference. It is not the literal taste of food discussed in the meaning-expansion lesson.

## 3. Desire and need

The source distinguishes wanting something from needing it.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is being described |
|---|---|---|
| want something | have a desire for it | The person wants it |
| need something for well-being | be in need of it | The person requires it |
| need more because there is not enough | be in need of more | The available amount is insufficient |

A **desire** is something a person wants. A **need** is something required for well-being or because the available amount is not enough.

Do not automatically replace every use of **want** with **need**. The source gives both expressions because the relation is different.

## 4. Keep neighbouring meanings in their own lessons

Some uses of the same words belong elsewhere:

- **feel a wall to see if it is dry** → put one's hands on it: physical touch, C02;
- **be conscious of something moving** → be conscious of it: knowledge/awareness, cross-linked to C05;
- **show interest** → give signs of it: already included in C01's show comparison, with an attitude/feeling link here;
- **show the effect of the past on the present** → make it clear: C01's show comparison, with a knowledge/understanding link to C05.

These examples are not missing from the course. They are kept in their primary lessons so C06 does not repeat C01, C02 or C05.

## 5. How to choose the expression

When you meet an inner-state or attitude word, ask:

1. **Is it an experience?** Pain, pleasure, an impression or anger?
2. **Is it liking a thing or a person?** Pleased with a thing, or friends with / getting on well with a person?
3. **Is it preference?** Having a taste for food or pictures?
4. **Is it approval?** Does the thing have our approval, or are we giving our approval to it?
5. **Is it desire or need?** Wanting something, or requiring it for well-being / because there is not enough?

The ordinary verb alone is not enough. Identify the relation before choosing the Basic expression.

## Practice

### A. Feeling and internal experience

Choose the Basic expression that matches the meaning.

1. A person feels pain.
   - A. have a feeling of pain
   - B. give approval
   - C. get facts into one's head

2. A person feels pleasure.
   - A. have a taste for it
   - B. have a feeling of pleasure
   - C. put one's hands on a wall

3. A person feels angry.
   - A. be feeling angry
   - B. have a taste for it
   - C. be in need of more

4. A person feels that something is so, but the sentence expresses an impression or idea.
   - A. have a feeling / idea that it is so
   - B. be certain of it
   - C. get facts into one's head

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source expresses pain as having a feeling of pain.
2. B — The source expresses pleasure as having a feeling of pleasure.
3. A — The source uses be feeling angry.
4. A — The source gives a feeling / idea that something is so; it is not automatically the same as certainty.

### B. Liking, friendship and preference

Match the situation with the Basic expression.

1. A person likes a thing.
2. A person likes another person.
3. A person likes good food or pictures.
4. A thing has the person's approval.

**Suggested answers**

1. be pleased with it;
2. be friends with them / get on well with them;
3. have a taste for them;
4. it has our approval.

### C. Personal liking and approval

Choose the expression that matches the relation.

1. A person likes a thing and has a positive personal response.
   - A. be pleased with it
   - B. have a taste for it
   - C. be in need of it

2. A thing has a person's approval.
   - A. it has our approval
   - B. get it in the hand
   - C. put it out of the mind

3. A person gives approval to a thing.
   - A. give our approval to it
   - B. be friends with them
   - C. have a desire for it

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — Be pleased with it describes a positive personal response.
2. A — The source says that something liked has our approval.
3. A — The source says that we give our approval to the thing.

### D. Desire and need

Choose the expression that preserves the relation.

1. A person wants a thing.
   - A. have a desire for it
   - B. have a taste for it
   - C. have no memory of it

2. A person requires something for well-being.
   - A. be in need of it
   - B. be pleased with it
   - C. get a grip of it

3. There is not enough of something, so more is required.
   - A. be in need of more
   - B. have a taste for it
   - C. give it a touch

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — Desire expresses wanting.
2. A — Need expresses something required for well-being.
3. A — The source gives be in need of more when the amount is insufficient.

### E. Keep the cross-topic boundaries clear

1. Someone feels a wall to test whether it is dry. Which lesson covers this physical meaning?

   **Answer:** C02 — Bodily Action & Contact. It is physical touch, not an internal feeling.

2. Someone is conscious of something moving. Which lesson covers this awareness expression?

   **Answer:** C05 — Thinking, Knowledge, Memory & Learning. It is awareness/knowledge, not the feeling rule in this lesson.

3. Someone shows interest by giving signs of it. Where is this source example already compared?

   **Answer:** C01's short show comparison. It links to attitude/feeling here; do not duplicate the show section.

4. Someone feels that something is so. Is this the same source expression as thinking that it is so?

   **Answer:** No. The source gives have a feeling / idea that it is so with the feeling examples; C05 separately gives be of the opinion / take the view that.

### F. Reverse understanding

Explain the relation described by each Basic expression:

1. have a feeling of pain;
2. have a feeling of pleasure;
3. have a feeling / idea that it is so;
4. be feeling angry;
5. be pleased with it;
6. be friends with them;
7. get on well with them;
8. have a taste for them;
9. it has our approval;
10. give our approval to it;
11. have a desire for it;
12. be in need of it;
13. be in need of more.

**Suggested answers**

1. Feel pain.
2. Feel pleasure.
3. Feel or have an impression that it is so.
4. Feel angry.
5. Like it / have a positive response to it.
6. Have a friendly relationship with them.
7. Have a good relationship with them.
8. Like or prefer the food or pictures.
9. We approve of the thing.
10. Express approval of it.
11. Want it.
12. Need it.
13. Need a larger amount because the available amount is insufficient.

### G. Independent construction

Choose one situation:

- feeling pain, pleasure, anger or an impression;
- liking a thing or having a friendly relationship with a person;
- a thing having our approval or giving approval to it;
- having a taste for food or pictures;
- wanting something or needing more of it.

State the relation first. Then choose the Basic expression that preserves whether it is an experience, preference, social relationship, approval, desire or need.
