# Module 2 · Lesson 7 — Exchange, Sale & Commitment

> **Draft status:** source-reviewed and internally reviewed teaching-content draft. It is based on Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English*, p.254, with selected examples from the 250 Fixed Word-Groups and the 50 Most Necessary groups. The fixed phrases are supporting contexts, not a second vocabulary lesson. All 35 closed practice items have matching answers; actual learner time and user feedback remain unmeasured.

## What this lesson teaches

When ordinary English describes buying, selling or making a promise, identify:

1. what is given;
2. what is received;
3. who gives and who receives it;
4. whether the exchange has happened or is only promised.

This lesson has three parts:

1. exchange and the buyer's / seller's viewpoints;
2. sale arrangements and conditions;
3. promises, agreements and business commitment.

The central idea is not a one-word replacement for buy or sell. It is describing the exchange from the correct person's viewpoint.

## 1. Buying and selling: two viewpoints of an exchange

The source explains buying and selling as two sides of the same transaction.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | Viewpoint |
|---|---|---|
| buy something | get it in exchange for money / at a price | The buyer gets the thing |
| buy something | give money for it | The buyer gives the money |
| sell something | get money for what one gives | The seller gets the money and gives the thing |

Keep the roles clear:

- **the buyer** gets the thing and gives money for it;
- **the seller** gives the thing and gets money for what is given.

Do not reverse the two viewpoints. If the sentence describes the buyer, ask what the buyer gets and what the buyer gives. If it describes the seller, ask what the seller gives and what the seller gets.

### Exchange does not always mean money

A fixed-group example describes taking a tin tray **in exchange for** a picture. One object is given for another. This is an exchange even though the example is not a purchase made with money.

So:

- **get it in exchange for money** describes a purchase;
- **take one thing in exchange for another** describes an exchange of objects.

The words around the exchange tell you what is given and what is received.

## 2. Sale arrangements and conditions

The source gives two ways of describing what a seller does with goods:

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| sell goods regularly | keep them in a store | Goods are kept ready for sale |
| sell what one makes | put it on the market | Goods are offered for sale |
| rent a house to a friend | let the house to a friend | The house is let for someone else's use |

These are different stages or forms of a commercial arrangement:

- **keep them in a store** describes holding goods for sale;
- **put it on the market** describes offering what has been made;
- **let a house to a friend** describes renting the house to that person. Here **let** is the fixed property use, not simply allowing someone to do something.

### Two different sale conditions

The fixed-group examples also use **on approval** and **on credit**.

| Fixed expression | Meaning in the source context | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| send a coat on approval | The coat is sent under an approval arrangement | It is not the same as saying that a person likes or approves of the coat |
| get a coat on credit | The coat is obtained with payment deferred | It is not the same as “to one's credit” |

Keep the difference:

- **on approval** concerns the sale arrangement and whether the item is approved;
- **on credit** concerns the payment arrangement.

The source does not turn these into a general lesson on contract law. They are short examples of how a sale may be arranged.

Do not treat **on credit** and **give one's word to do so** as the same expression:

- **on credit** names the purchase/payment condition;
- **give one's word to do so** names a person's commitment to give the money later.

## 3. Promises, agreements and business commitment

The source gives a clear difference between receiving money now and promising to give it later.

If we have no money, we may promise to give it later by **giving our word to do so**. Here **promise** names the ordinary-English meaning; the Basic expression is **give one's word to do so**.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source or fixed-group example | What is described |
|---|---|---|
| promise to give money later | give one's word to do so | A commitment about a future action |
| keep a promise | keep one's word | Carry out the commitment already made |
| reach an agreement about a matter | come to agreement | The people or groups reach a shared arrangement |
| complete a business undertaking | put the business through | The undertaking is carried through to completion |
| take part in a company's organization | take part in the organization of the company | Participation in a business arrangement |

A promise is not the same as payment:

- **give one's word to do so** means that the person commits to giving the money later;
- it does not mean that the money has already been given;
- **keep one's word** concerns carrying out the promise.

The fixed-group examples also mention a chance of profit when someone takes part in the organization of a company. This is a business context, not a new rule about buying or selling.

## 4. Business context: result and demand

A few fixed-group examples give useful business context:

- **the profit / loss / interest on a business** describes a financial result or return connected with that business;
- the small slips in the source were **much in request**; this means people were asking for them or wanted them;
- **take part in the organization of a company** describes participation in a business.

These are supporting examples. They do not create separate lessons on accounting, company law or finance.

## 5. Keep similar-looking expressions separate

Some fixed expressions contain the same words but describe different meanings.

### Credit

- **get something on credit**: obtain it with payment deferred;
- **to one's credit**: something is a point in that person's favour or merit.

The second is about reputation or merit, not a purchase.

### Approval

- **send a coat on approval**: a sale arrangement;
- **the thing has our approval**: a person's positive judgment, taught in C06.

The shared word does not make the two uses the same lesson.

### Loss

- **the loss on a business**: a financial result;
- **be at a loss for something to do**: be uncertain about what action to take.

The second is a knowledge/uncertainty use, not a financial loss.

### Finding and discovery

The same source passage also gives examples of finding money, seeing that free meals are being given, discovering new land, and losing something. They do not all describe a sale:

- coming across money by chance → C04, encounter/location;
- seeing that meals are being given → C01, perception/information;
- making a discovery of new land → C05, knowledge;
- being unable to put one's hands on something / having no idea where it is → C04/C05 according to whether the focus is location or knowledge;
- something that was taken has gone for ever → C04, movement/result.

They are kept in their primary lessons rather than added to C07.

## 6. How to choose the expression

When you meet a buying, selling or commitment sentence, ask:

1. **Who is the person in the sentence?** Buyer, seller, owner, customer or someone making a promise?
2. **What is given?** The goods, money, another object, or one's word?
3. **What is received?** Goods, money, another object, or a future commitment?
4. **Has the exchange happened?** Or is it only promised or arranged?
5. **What is the sale condition?** On approval, on credit, or an ordinary exchange?
6. **Is it really a transaction?** A phrase about reputation, uncertainty, finding or discovery may belong elsewhere.

Do not start by replacing buy, sell or promise with one fixed verb. Identify the roles and the status of the exchange first.

## Practice

### A. Identify the buyer's and seller's viewpoints

Choose the expression that matches the situation.

1. A buyer gets a thing and gives money for it.
   - A. get it in exchange for money / give money for it
   - B. get money for what one gives
   - C. put it on the market

2. A seller gives a thing and receives money for it.
   - A. give money for it
   - B. get money for what one gives
   - C. keep it in mind

3. A person gives a picture and receives a tin tray in exchange.
   - A. take a tin tray in exchange for the picture
   - B. get the tray on credit
   - C. keep the tray in a store

4. A buyer has no money now and commits to giving it later.
   - A. give one's word to do so
   - B. put it on the market
   - C. have a taste for it

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The buyer gets the thing and gives money for it.
2. B — The seller gets money for what is given.
3. A — One object is given in exchange for another.
4. A — The source describes the future payment as giving one's word to do so.

### B. Identify the sale arrangement

Choose the expression that preserves the meaning.

1. A seller keeps goods ready because they are sold regularly.
   - A. keep them in a store
   - B. give one's word
   - C. come to agreement

2. A maker offers the things made for sale.
   - A. put them on the market
   - B. get money for what one gives
   - C. be in need of more

3. A person rents a house to a friend.
   - A. let the house to a friend
   - B. get the house on credit
   - C. take a house in exchange for a picture

4. A coat is sent under an approval arrangement.
   - A. send it on approval
   - B. send it on credit
   - C. put it out of the mind

5. A person obtains a coat with payment deferred.
   - A. get it on credit
   - B. send it on approval
   - C. keep it in a store

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — Goods are kept in a store ready for sale.
2. A — What is made is put on the market.
3. A — Let a house to someone is the source's fixed expression for renting it to that person.
4. A — On approval is the sale arrangement in the source's coat example.
5. A — On credit is the purchase/payment condition in the source's coat example.

### C. Distinguish promise, agreement and completion

Choose the expression that matches the situation.

1. Governments reach an agreed arrangement about taxes.
   - A. come to agreement
   - B. put it on the market
   - C. get money for what one gives

2. A person commits to giving money later.
   - A. give one's word to do so
   - B. get it on credit
   - C. keep it in a store

3. A person carries a business undertaking through to completion.
   - A. put the business through
   - B. put it on approval
   - C. come across it

4. A person carries out a promise already made.
   - A. keep one's word
   - B. give money for it
   - C. let a house

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source says that governments come to agreement about taxes.
2. A — Giving one's word is the commitment to give the money later.
3. A — Put the business through describes carrying the undertaking through.
4. A — Keeping one's word means carrying out the promise.

### D. Understand the business context

Explain what each fixed expression describes.

1. take part in the organization of a company;
2. the profit / loss / interest on a business;
3. the small slips made for weighing are much in request.

**Suggested answers**

1. Participate in the organization of the company.
2. A financial result or return connected with the business.
3. The slips are wanted or asked for by people.

### E. Keep the boundaries clear

1. A coat is sent on approval. Is this the same as saying that a person has approval of the coat?

   **Answer:** No. On approval is a sale arrangement; having approval of something is a person's judgment, covered in C06.

2. Someone gets a coat on credit. Does this mean the coat is “to that person's credit”?

   **Answer:** No. On credit concerns deferred payment. To one's credit describes merit or something in a person's favour.

3. Someone is at a loss for something to do. Is this a financial loss?

   **Answer:** No. It means the person is uncertain about what action to take; this belongs with C05.

4. Someone comes across money in the street. Is this a buying or selling event?

   **Answer:** No. It describes an accidental encounter/location, covered in C04.

### F. Reverse understanding

Explain what each Basic expression says about the transaction or commitment.

1. get it in exchange for money;
2. give money for it;
3. get money for what one gives;
4. keep goods in a store;
5. put what one makes on the market;
6. let a house to a friend;
7. send a coat on approval;

9. give one's word to do so;
10. keep one's word;
11. come to agreement;
12. put the business through;
13. take one object in exchange for another;
14. take part in the organization of a company;
15. small slips much in request.

**Suggested answers**

1. The buyer receives the thing and gives money for it.
2. The buyer gives money to receive the thing.
3. The seller receives money for the thing given.
4. Goods are kept ready for sale.
5. Goods made by the seller are offered for sale.
6. The house is rented to the friend.
7. The coat is sent under an approval arrangement.
8. The coat is obtained with payment deferred.
9. The person commits to doing something later.
10. The person carries out the promise already made.
11. The people reach an agreed arrangement.
12. The business undertaking is carried through to completion.
13. One object is given and another is received.
14. The person participates in organizing the company.
15. The small slips are wanted or requested by people.

### G. Independent construction

Choose one situation:

- buying something and giving money for it;
- selling something and receiving money;
- offering goods for sale;
- renting a house to someone;
- obtaining something on credit or receiving it on approval;
- making a promise to give money later;
- carrying out a business undertaking.

First identify who gives what and who gets what. Then choose the Basic expression that preserves whether the transaction has happened, is arranged, or is only promised.

## Lesson review

You should now be able to:

- describe buying from the buyer's viewpoint and selling from the seller's viewpoint;
- distinguish an exchange of objects from a purchase for money;
- distinguish keeping goods in a store from putting goods on the market;
- distinguish on approval from on credit;
- distinguish a promise from payment and keeping one's word from giving one's word;
- recognize the selected business fixed phrases without confusing them with unrelated meanings such as “to one's credit” or “at a loss”.
