# Module 1 · Lesson 2 — Names of Qualities: Meaning Expansion & Use as Things

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on C. K. Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English* (1932), especially pp.152 and 160. The lesson uses selected teaching examples and preserves the complete source-listed Quality-as-Thing group as a reference. Clarity, exercise quality and actual learner time still require review.

## What you will learn

A Quality word normally tells us what a person or thing is like. The source describes two further uses:

1. A Quality can extend from a physical property to a related experience, response or judgment.
2. A Quality word can sometimes be used as a **Name of Things**.

These are two different operations. In the first, the word still describes a Quality, but its meaning has been extended. In the second, the word itself names something.

Do not mix either operation with Special Use / meaning narrowing, which is taught in the next lesson.

## 1. From a physical Quality to a human experience or response

The source begins with four comparisons:

| Familiar use | Extended use | What connects them |
|---|---|---|
| a bitter taste | a bitter experience | an unpleasant taste and an unpleasant experience can produce a related reaction |
| a cold day | a cold look | cold weather can make the body stiff; a cold look can make a person's behavior stiff |
| a smooth floor | smooth words | a smooth floor is kind to the feet; smooth words can be kind to a person's feelings |
| a bright colour | a bright face | a bright colour can make us happy; a bright face can produce a similar response |

The extended use does not mean that an experience literally has a taste, a look literally has a temperature, or words have a smooth physical surface.

The source is showing how a Quality can move from a physical property to a human reaction or experience.

## 2. Extension through a commonly associated Quality

The source gives two further examples. They are not based on a direct physical resemblance.

### cheap

Cheap things are often thought to be in bad taste. The source therefore records a use in which something in bad taste can be called **cheap**, even when a high price was paid for it.

In this use, cheap is not reporting the price. It is expressing the associated judgment about taste.

### simple

An act that is less complex is less hard to do. An act that gives us little trouble can therefore be called **simple**.

Here, simple describes how easy the act is to do, not merely how plain an object looks.

### The limit

These are recognized extensions based on an association or tendency. They are not universal laws:

- a low price does not prove that something is in bad taste;
- a high price does not prevent the source's extended use of cheap;
- not every easy thing must be described by every possible sense of simple.

The learner must understand the particular use in context, rather than turn the association into an unlimited rule.

## 3. Quality words used as Names of Things

This is a different operation.

A Quality word usually describes another Name:

- a sweet fruit;
- a quiet room;
- a solid object.

In the source-listed Quality-as-Thing use, the Quality word itself functions as a Name. It can refer to a substance, person, thing, time, relation or another named item, depending on the word and context.

A few examples help show the change:

| Quality word | Use as a Name of Things |
|---|---|
| acid | a substance |
| chemical | a substance |
| chief | a person or leading member |
| female / male | a person or member of a sex |
| first / last / second | a person or thing in an order |
| past / present / future | a period or part of time |
| opposite | the thing or position opposed to another |
| colour words | the colours themselves |

These groupings are a teaching aid. They are not formal categories supplied by Ogden, and they do not mean that every Quality can be used in the same way.

### The complete source list

The source lists these 35 Quality forms, as well as colour words:

acid, chief, chemical, cold, complex, cut, elastic, equal, fat, female, first, flat, future, good(s), hollow, last, living, male, natural, opposite, parallel, past, present, public, quiet, right, safe, same, second, secret, solid, sweet, waiting, wrong, young.

This is a bounded source list. Do not infer that every modern English adjective can automatically become a Basic Name.

### Five forms with a specific restriction

The source explicitly says that these forms are used only with **the** in this Quality-as-Thing use:

- dark;
- first;
- last;
- quiet;
- same.

This restriction belongs to these listed uses. Do not turn it into a claim that every Quality-as-Thing form must always have the.

## 4. Keep the two operations separate

| Example | Operation | What the word is doing |
|---|---|---|
| a bitter taste | familiar Quality | describing a taste |
| a bitter experience | meaning expansion | describing an experience through a related human reaction |
| a cold day | familiar Quality | describing temperature |
| a cold look | meaning expansion | describing a look through its effect or attitude |
| the past | Quality used as a Name | naming a period of time |
| the first | Quality used as a Name | naming the first person or thing in an order |

The question is not simply “Does the word have another meaning?” Ask what the word is doing in this sentence:

- Is it still describing a Quality, with an extended sense?
- Or is it functioning as a Name of Things?

## Practice

### A. Recognize the Quality extension

Choose the best explanation.

1. **A bitter experience**
   - A. An unpleasant experience is described through a related reaction to bitter taste.
   - B. The experience literally has a taste.
   - C. The word describes a person's position in a sequence.

2. **A cold look**
   - A. A look has a low physical temperature.
   - B. A look or attitude is described through a related human response.
   - C. The word names a period of time.

3. **Smooth words**
   - A. Words have a smooth physical surface.
   - B. Words are described through the effect they have on feelings.
   - C. The words are made from a smooth material.

4. **A bright face**
   - A. The face is literally a bright colour.
   - B. The face produces a response related to the response produced by a bright colour.
   - C. The face is a colour name used as a thing.

5. Something is called **cheap** because it is in bad taste, although it cost a great deal.
   - A. The word is reporting its price.
   - B. The word is using the source-listed extended sense.
   - C. The word is naming a person.

6. An act that gives us little trouble is called **simple**.
   - A. Simple describes how easy the act is to do.
   - B. Simple means that the act has no meaning.
   - C. Simple is being used as a time word.

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source connects bitter taste with the unpleasant reaction of a bitter experience.
2. B — The extended use concerns the look or attitude, not its physical temperature.
3. B — Smooth words are compared with the way a smooth floor feels to the feet.
4. B — A bright face can make us happy in a way compared with a bright colour.
5. B — The source records cheap as a judgment about bad taste, even when the price is high.
6. A — The source connects a less complex act with an act that is less hard to do.

### B. Recognize a Quality used as a Name

For each item, identify what the Quality word is naming.

1. **a chemical**
2. **the chief**
3. **the past**
4. **the first**
5. **the opposite**
6. a colour word used as the name of that colour

**Suggested answers**

1. A substance.
2. A person or leading member.
3. A period of time.
4. The first person or thing in an order.
5. The thing or position opposed to another.
6. The colour itself.

The important point is that the word is functioning as a Name, not merely describing a separate thing.

### C. Apply the source restriction

When these words are used as Names of Things in the source-listed way, which form must be used?

- dark
- first
- last
- quiet
- same

**Answer:** all five are used only with **the**: the dark, the first, the last, the quiet, the same.

This is a specific source restriction. It does not mean every word in the complete Quality-as-Thing list follows the same article rule.

### D. Distinguish the two operations

Classify each use:

1. a cold day;
2. a cold look;
3. the past;
4. the first.

**Answers**

1. Familiar Quality: cold describes temperature.
2. Meaning expansion: cold describes a look through a related response or attitude.
3. Quality used as a Name: past names a period of time.
4. Quality used as a Name: first names a person or thing in an order.

### E. Reverse check

1. In **smooth words**, what is being described: the physical surface of the words, or their effect on a person's feelings?
2. In **the past**, is past describing another Name, or naming a period of time?

**Answers**

1. Their effect on a person's feelings.
2. It is naming a period of time.

## Lesson review

You should now be able to:

- recognize a Quality extended to a related human experience or response;
- explain the source's cheap and simple examples without turning them into universal rules;
- recognize the source-listed Quality forms used as Names of Things;
- apply the special the restriction to dark, first, last, quiet and same;
- distinguish meaning expansion from a Quality word functioning as a Name.
