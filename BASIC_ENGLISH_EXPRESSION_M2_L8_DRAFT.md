# Module 2 · Lesson 8 — State and Process: Change, Entry & Ending

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on *The ABC of Basic English*, printed pp.255–256, and selected Fixed Word-Groups. The source's use of the ordinary-English word **finish** is explicitly not treated as a Basic expression. Source distinctions, closed answer keys and cross-topic boundaries are included; actual learner time and user feedback remain unmeasured.

## What this lesson teaches

This lesson is not a list of verbs with one replacement each. The learner identifies what is happening:

1. a Quality is changing;
2. a process is under way;
3. something is entering a state;
4. something is already in a state;
5. an event takes place;
6. an activity or process comes to an end.

The same ordinary-English word can describe different relations. Choose the Basic expression only after identifying the relation.

## 1. A change in Quality or a process under way

The source gives four examples of a change in Quality:

| Ordinary-English meaning | Basic expression in the source | What it describes |
|---|---|---|
| boys and girls grow taller | get taller | A change in height |
| trees grow higher | get higher | A change in height |
| old men grow fatter | get fatter | A change in size |
| a person's powers grow greater | get greater | An increase in degree or strength |

The source then gives a different expression for things that are increasing in these ways:

| Ordinary-English meaning | Basic expression in the source | What it describes |
|---|---|---|
| be growing | be in the process of growth or development | The process is under way |

Keep the two constructions distinct:

- **get + Quality** describes a change into a Quality: get taller, get higher, get fatter, get greater;
- **be in the process of + Name** describes the process that is under way: be in the process of growth or development.

These are the source's examples. Do not turn them into a rule that every use of grow must be translated by get, or that every ongoing action must use be in the process of.

## 2. Entering a state or being in that state

The source explains sleep with two expressions:

| Ordinary-English meaning | Basic expression in the source | What it describes |
|---|---|---|
| go to sleep | go to sleep | Entering the state of sleep |
| be asleep / be in sleep | be in sleep | Being in the state of sleep |

The decision is:

- **go to sleep** — the change into the state;
- **be in sleep** — the state itself.

Keep the exact source expression **be in sleep** when studying this Basic example. It is not a reason to replace every sentence containing sleep with the same phrase.

## 3. Expressing the end of a life

The source gives three expressions for die:

- go to one's death;
- come to one's end;
- take one's last breath.

All three describe the end of a life. They are alternative source expressions, not three unrelated grammar rules.

Do not replace the exact phrase **come to one's end** with **come to an end** and then assume the source has supplied a general translation for every kind of ending. The example on this page is about death.

## 4. Beginning, and the source boundary around finish

The source gives:

- begin → make a start.

There is an important boundary here. The source also says, in its explanatory English, that everything that begins will later finish. It then uses **make a start** to describe beginning the physical acts.

That sentence does **not** give a Basic equivalent for the general verb **finish**. The word finish is not one of the 850 base words. Therefore:

- **make a start** is the source-provided Basic expression for begin;
- **finish → finish** is not a valid Basic mapping from this passage;
- this lesson does not invent a universal Basic replacement for finish.

The lesson still teaches ending and cessation through the source's death expressions and the fixed groups below. It does not pretend that the source has provided a general finish formula.

## 5. Fixed groups that reinforce state, change and cessation

The 50 Most Necessary Fixed Word-Groups contain several expressions that reinforce this lesson:

| Fixed group | Relation | What the learner should notice |
|---|---|---|
| in motion | State / activity | Something is moving |
| in use / operation | State / activity | Something is being used or is operating |
| go to sleep | Entry into a state | A person enters the state of sleep |
| lights go out | Change of state | The lights stop shining |
| come to a stop | Reaching a stopped state | The subject reaches a stopping point |
| put a stop to an activity | Causing cessation | Someone or something causes the activity to stop |
| take place | Event occurrence | An event happens |

Two expressions about stopping must not be collapsed:

- **come to a stop** describes the subject reaching a stopped state;
- **put a stop to** describes causing an activity or process to stop.

**In the end** is primarily a C11 Time & Sequence item. It may be cross-linked here when the context is about the eventual outcome of a process, but it is not a core C08 state/process expression.

These fixed groups are selected reinforcement. They do not create a second vocabulary lesson.

## 6. How to choose the expression

When the ordinary-English sentence concerns growth, sleep, death, beginning or stopping, ask:

1. Is a Quality changing, or is a process under way?
2. Is something entering a state, or is it already in that state?
3. Is the sentence about the end of a life?
4. Is it about beginning? If so, use the source's **make a start** expression.
5. Is an event taking place, or is an activity being brought to a stop?
6. Is the phrase **in the end** being used for time/sequence? If so, keep its main teaching home in C11.

Do not choose a phrase just because it contains a similar word. Preserve the relation in the sentence.

## Practice

### A. Change, process, entry and state

Choose the expression that preserves the meaning.

1. A boy grows taller.
   - A. get taller
   - B. be in the process of growth
   - C. go to sleep

2. A tree is in the middle of growing.
   - A. get higher
   - B. be in the process of growth
   - C. come to a stop

3. A child is entering the state of sleep.
   - A. be in sleep
   - B. go to sleep
   - C. take place

4. A person is already in the state of sleep.
   - A. go to sleep
   - B. be in sleep
   - C. make a start

5. A person dies. Which expressions does the source give?
   - A. go to one's death / come to one's end / take one's last breath
   - B. get taller / get higher
   - C. in motion / in use

6. A person begins a task.
   - A. make a start
   - B. put a stop to it
   - C. be in sleep

7. A person finishes a task. Does this passage give a Basic equivalent for the general meaning of finish?
   - A. Yes: finish → finish
   - B. No: finish occurs in explanatory English, but no Basic equivalent is supplied here

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source uses get + Quality for the change in height.
2. B — The source uses be in the process of growth or development for the process under way.
3. B — Go to sleep describes entering the state.
4. B — Be in sleep describes being in the state.
5. A — These are the three expressions the source gives for die.
6. A — The source gives make a start for begin.
7. B — The sentence with finish is explanatory English. It is not a Basic translation example, and finish is not in the 850 base list.

### B. State, event and stopping fixed groups

Match the situation with the fixed group.

1. A machine is operating.
2. A thing is moving.
3. The lights stop shining.
4. A moving thing reaches a stopped state.
5. Someone causes an activity to stop.
6. An event happens.
7. A story uses in the end to show its final temporal outcome.

**Suggested answers**

2. in motion;
3. lights go out;
4. come to a stop;
5. put a stop to the activity;
6. take place;
7. in the end — its primary lesson is C11 Time & Sequence; cross-link to C08 only when discussing the eventual outcome of a process.
