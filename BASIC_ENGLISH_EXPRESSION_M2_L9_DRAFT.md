# Module 2 · Lesson 9 — Ability, Obligation & Intention

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on *The ABC of Basic English*, printed p.256, and the accepted C09 teaching flow. The source-specific meanings of can, ought and shall are kept distinct. The fixed group be able to reinforces only the ability meaning. Source/answer coverage is included; actual learner time and user feedback remain unmeasured.

## What this lesson teaches

The source treats **can**, **ought** and **shall** as ordinary-English words that need special handling when we express an action in Basic English. They are not three Basic words with one fixed Chinese meaning each.

The learner must identify the relation expressed:

1. ability — a person is able to do something;
2. possibility — it is possible for the person to do it;
3. advisability — it is right or wise to do it;
4. duty or responsibility — it is the person's business to do it;
5. probability — the facts support an expectation;
6. intention or decision — the speaker asks whether they are going to do something.

The question is not simply “Which modal verb is this?” It is “What does the speaker mean about the action?”

## 1. can — ability or possibility

The source gives two expressions:

| Meaning of can | Basic expression in the source | What it describes |
|---|---|---|
| I can do it — I have the ability | I am able to do it | The person's ability or capacity |
| I can do it — it is possible for me | It is possible for me to do it | The possibility of doing it |

Do not treat these as identical:

- **be able to do it** describes ability;
- **it is possible for me to do it** describes possibility.

The same ordinary-English sentence may need context before we know which relation is intended. Do not choose one expression merely because the word can appears.

## 2. ought — right/wise, duty, or probability

The source gives three distinct meanings.

### A. Right or wise

If the intended meaning is that an action is right or wise, use:

- it is right for me to do it;
- it is wise for me to do it.

This is a judgment about whether the action is the right or wise thing to do.

### B. A person's business or duty

If the intended meaning is that the action is the person's responsibility, use:

- it is my business to do it.

This is not merely a suggestion that the action is wise. It expresses responsibility or duty.

### C. Probability from a fact

The source gives this example:

- ordinary English: the train ought to be here in half an hour;
- Basic explanation: it is probable from the fact that it will be so.

Here ought does not express a duty for the train. It expresses an expectation based on the fact given.

Keep these three readings separate:

| Intended force | Basic expression |
|---|---|
| right / wise | it is right / wise for me to do it |
| duty / responsibility | it is my business to do it |
| probability from evidence | it is probable from the fact that it will be so |

Do not automatically read every use of ought as an order or obligation.

## 3. shall — statement or question

The source makes a clear distinction between statements and questions.

### A. Shall in a statement

In a statement that is not a question, the source says shall is covered by **will**.

Project-created illustration:

- ordinary English: I shall go;
- Basic expression: I will go.

This is the statement use. Do not apply it mechanically to questions.

### B. “Shall I do it?”

The source gives two possible meanings:

- **Am I going to do it or not?** — asking about intention or decision;
- **Am I wise to do it?** — asking whether the action is wise.

The context determines which question is intended. Do not force one reading onto every use of “Shall I ...?”

## 4. Fixed-group reinforcement: be able to

The 50 Most Necessary Fixed Word-Groups include **be able to**.

Use it to reinforce the ability meaning of can:

- can do it (ability) → be able to do it.

It does not replace the possibility meaning of can, the duty or probability meanings of ought, or the statement/question distinction of shall.

The fixed group supports the lesson; it is not a separate modal rule.

## 5. Keep condition separate

The source also explains **unless** as **if ... not**. This is a conditional-clause relation, not one of the can / ought / shall meanings.

- unless I do it → if I do not do it.

This belongs to the existing conditional/sentence-structure foundation. C09 may use it as a boundary check, but does not reteach conditional sentences.

## 6. How to choose the expression

When you meet can, ought or shall, ask:

1. **can:** Is the sentence about a person's ability, or about what is possible for the person?
2. **ought:** Is it right/wise, the person's business/duty, or probable from a fact?
3. **shall:** Is it a statement or a question?
4. **Shall I ...?:** Is the speaker asking about intention, or whether the action is wise?
5. **unless:** Is this a conditional relation that belongs in sentence structure?

Identify the force before choosing the Basic expression.

## Practice

### A. can — ability or possibility

Choose the expression that preserves the intended meaning.

1. A person has the ability to do the action.
   - A. be able to do it
   - B. it is probable from the fact that it will be so
   - C. it is my business to do it

2. The intended meaning is that it is possible for the person to do the action.
   - A. be able to do it
   - B. it is possible for me to do it
   - C. it is right for me to do it

3. Someone says, “I can do it,” but gives no further context. What must the learner check?
   - A. Whether the sentence means ability or possibility
   - B. Whether the sentence means a duty
   - C. Whether it is a question with shall

4. What does the fixed group be able to directly reinforce?
   - A. Ability
   - B. Every meaning of can
   - C. Probability

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — Be able to expresses the person's ability.
2. B — It is possible for me to do it expresses possibility.
3. A — The source gives both ability and possibility readings for can. Context selects the intended relation.
4. A — Be able to reinforces ability; it does not replace the possibility reading.

### B. ought — right/wise, duty, or probability

Choose the meaning that matches the context.

1. The intended meaning is that doing the action is right or wise.
   - A. it is right / wise for me to do it
   - B. it is probable from the fact that it will be so
   - C. I will do it

2. The intended meaning is that the action is the person's responsibility.
   - A. it is possible for me to do it
   - B. it is my business to do it
   - C. I am going to do it or not

3. The train ought to be here in half an hour. Which meaning does the source explain?
   - A. The train has a duty to arrive.
   - B. It is probable from the fact that it will be so.
   - C. It is wise for the train to arrive.

4. Does every use of ought express a duty?
   - A. Yes, it always expresses duty.
   - B. No. It may express right/wisdom, duty, or probability from a fact.

5. A statement is about what is expected because of a known fact. Which ought reading is intended?
   - A. Probability from evidence
   - B. A person's duty
   - C. A question about intention

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — This is the source's right/wise meaning.
2. B — The source uses “it is my business to do it” for the duty/responsibility meaning.
3. B — The train example is an expectation based on the fact, not a duty imposed on the train.
4. B — The source gives three meanings; they must not be collapsed into duty.
5. A — The expectation is based on a fact, so this is the probability reading.

### C. shall — statement or question

Choose the Basic expression that matches the sentence.

1. Shall is used in a statement, not a question.
   - A. will
   - B. am I wise to do it?
   - C. it is my business to do it

2. “Shall I do it?” is asking whether the speaker is going to do it or not.
   - A. Am I going to do it or not?
   - B. Am I wise to do it?
   - C. It is probable from the fact that it will be so.

3. “Shall I do it?” is asking whether the action is wise.
   - A. Am I going to do it or not?
   - B. Am I wise to do it?
   - C. I am able to do it.

4. Without context, should every “Shall I ...?” question be assigned only one of these meanings?
   - A. Yes, it always asks about intention.
   - B. No. It may ask about intention or whether the action is wise.

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — In non-question statements, the source says shall is covered by will.
2. A — This is the intention/decision reading.
3. B — This is the advisability reading.
4. B — The source gives both readings; the context determines which one is intended.

### D. Keep the boundary clear

1. A sentence uses unless. Which foundation handles it?
   
   **Answer:** Conditional sentence structure. Unless is expressed with if and not; it is not one of the C09 modal meanings.

2. A learner uses be able to. Which C09 meaning does this reinforce?
   
   **Answer:** Ability. It does not automatically express possibility, duty or probability.

### E. Reverse understanding

Explain the force described by each Basic expression.

1. be able to do it;
2. it is possible for me to do it;
3. it is right for me to do it;
4. it is wise for me to do it;
5. it is my business to do it;
6. it is probable from the fact that it will be so;
7. will in a statement;
8. Am I going to do it or not?
9. Am I wise to do it?
10. if I do not do it.

**Suggested answers**

1. The person has the ability to do it.
2. It is possible for the person to do it.
3. Doing it is the right thing.
4. Doing it is wise.
5. It is the person's duty or responsibility.
6. The facts support the expectation that it will be so.
7. The statement use of shall.
8. A question about intention or decision.
9. A question about whether the action is wise.
10. The conditional expression used for unless.

### F. Independent construction

Choose one situation:

- a person has the ability to do something;
- an action is possible for a person;
- an action is right or wise;
- an action is someone's responsibility;
- an event is probable from a known fact;
- a statement uses shall;
- a question with “Shall I ...?” asks about intention;
- a question with “Shall I ...?” asks whether an action is wise.

First identify the force. Then choose the Basic expression that preserves it.

## Lesson review

You should now be able to:

- distinguish ability from possibility in can;
- distinguish right/wisdom, duty/responsibility and probability from a fact in ought;
- use will for shall in statements;
- distinguish the two readings of “Shall I ...?”;
- use be able to as an ability expression without treating it as a replacement for every modal meaning;
- keep unless with conditional sentence structure.
