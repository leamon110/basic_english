# Basic English Expression System — Accepted Curriculum Architecture

> **Status: structure-level architecture accepted after source-coverage, prerequisite, progression and first teaching-flow audits.**
>
> This document fixes the current Module / Lesson architecture. It does not claim that lesson scripts, exercises, App screens or final durations are complete. Those are the next phase.

## 1. Final structure at the curriculum-architecture level

<summary></summary>

- **2 Modules**
- **15 Lessons**
  - Module 1: 3 Lessons
  - Module 2: 12 Lessons

The count comes from the learner's distinct decision tasks and the tested teaching-flow boundaries. It is not a target derived from the number of source chapters or vocabulary items.

### Numbering — important distinction

- **Module** is the top-level division: there are exactly two, Module 1 and Module 2.
- **Lesson numbers restart inside each Module**: Module 1 has Lessons 1–3; Module 2 has Lessons 1–12.
- **C01–C15 are internal Candidate IDs** retained from the research/candidate-structure documents. They are not Module numbers, global Lesson numbers or final App screen IDs.
- Mapping: **C01–C12 = Module 2 Lessons 1–12**; **C13–C15 = Module 1 Lessons 1–3**. The C numbers preserve the order in which candidate topics were tracked; they do not determine the final teaching order.
- In learner-facing discussion and App planning, use **Module 1 · Lesson 1** or **Module 2 · Lesson 1**. Add the C ID in parentheses only when cross-referencing research files (for example, Module 2 · Lesson 3 (C03)).

## 2. Module 1 — Understanding Basic Word Meanings

**Module objective:** understand how a known Basic word can refer to a related meaning, function as another word class in source-listed cases, or be narrowed by context.

| Lesson | Title | Main learning task |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Names of Things — Meaning Expansion** | Identify the source-supported relation that lets a Name refer to another related object, feature or situation |
| 2 | **Names of Qualities — Meaning Expansion & Use as Things** | Understand Quality extensions and recognize the source-listed Quality forms that function as Names of Things |
| 3 | **Special Use — Meaning Narrowing** | Use context to identify a more specific referent within a broader word meaning |

### Module 1 boundaries

- Lesson 1 covers Names of Things relations: body/structure, sensory, function/behavior, “as if”, cause/process, use, part/whole, substance/object and other source-recognized connections.
- Lesson 2 keeps two parts explicit: Quality meaning extension; Quality words used as Names of Things. It preserves the source's restriction that dark, first, last, quiet and same occur only with “the”.
- Lesson 3 covers material-name uses, important subclasses and contextually expected narrower senses.
- Meaning expansion and meaning narrowing are not the same operation.
- Quality-as-Thing is not the same as Special Use / narrowing.
- The 250 Fixed Word-Groups' idiomatic uses are not part of Lesson 3.

**Order:** Things expansion → Qualities expansion/function → Special Use/narrowing. This moves from related referents to Quality uses, then contrasts widening with contextual narrowing.

## 3. Module 2 — Expressing Common Meanings in Basic English

**Module objective:** start from an ordinary-English meaning, identify the actual sense in context, construct or select a Basic expression, and check that the original meaning is preserved.

| Lesson | Title | Main learning task |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Communication & Information** | Identify the communication event, roles, medium and destination |
| 2 | **Bodily Action & Contact** | Express bodily intake/expulsion and physical contact/impact |
| 3 | **Physical Effects: Damage, Change & Result** | Distinguish an action, its physical effect, the resulting state and self-occurring change |
| 4 | **Movement & Position: Transfer, Posture & Viewpoint** | Express object transfer, path/destination, viewpoint and body position |
| 5 | **Thinking, Knowledge, Memory & Learning** | Express mental activity, judgment, knowledge, understanding, memory and knowledge acquisition |
| 6 | **Feeling, Preference, Desire & Need** | Express internal experience, attitude, preference, desire and need |
| 7 | **Exchange, Sale & Commitment** | Express transactions, sale arrangements, payment/value and promises |
| 8 | **State and Process: Change, Entry & Ending** | Express a state, entering a state, an ongoing process, beginning and ending |
| 9 | **Ability, Obligation & Intention** | Distinguish ability, possibility, duty, advice, probability and intention |
| 10 | **Quantity, Amount & Degree** | Express distribution, totality, amount and degree |
| 11 | **Time & Sequence** | Express past time, time up to now, starting points, sequence, repetition and continuation |
| 12 | **Context-Dependent Words & Paraphrase** | Interpret a non-Basic ordinary-English word in context and describe its meaning with Basic words |

### Module 2 boundaries

- Lesson 1 groups expressing, receiving and written/transmitted information. All show senses appear only in a short comparison panel to show how one ordinary word changes meaning; they are not all classified as communication.
- Lesson 2 teaches the physical action itself. Lesson 3 teaches damage/effect/result. Do not merge contact with the result it may cause.
- Lesson 4 teaches spatial movement and posture. It uses Directives as foundation tools rather than reteaching them.
- Lesson 5 is cognition/knowledge; Lesson 6 is feeling/attitude/desire. Keep physical touch under feel cross-linked to Lesson 2.
- Lesson 7 is commercial exchange/commitment. Its selected fixed-group examples reinforce agreement, renting, credit, exchange, business and keeping one's word; do not pad it with unrelated social phrases.
- Lesson 8 is state/process; Lesson 9 is the speaker's stance toward an action or event.
- Lesson 10 is amount/degree; Lesson 11 is time/sequence. Keep the time sense of once cross-linked from Lesson 10.
- Lesson 12 starts from an ordinary-English word outside the Basic set and paraphrases it. Lesson 3 of Module 1 starts from a Basic word and narrows its referent. These are opposite task directions.

## 4. Foundation and prerequisite rules

The Expression System applies existing foundations; it does not recreate them as separate Expression Lessons.

| Foundation / prerequisite | How the Expression course uses it |
|---|---|
| 850-word vocabulary and word classes | Assumed throughout |
| 16 core Operators | Used to build expressions; their basic meanings are taught in Structured Teaching |
| 20 proper Directives and related word combinations | Used in spatial, relational and abstract expressions; not retaught as a separate grammar course |
| Names of Acts / sentence roles | Reused across Module 2 |
| Names of Directions | Reused especially in Module 2 Lessons 2–4 |
| -er / -ing / -ed and other basic word forms | Remain in the word-form foundation; selected meanings are reinforced where needed |
| Fixed Word-Groups | Phrase-level reinforcement distributed by meaning |
| Meaning → Basic method | One shared method; each Lesson practises its own semantic decision |

### Two explicit local rules

**Module 2 Lesson 3 — Physical Effects**

- Prerequisite: current App Step 17, sections 4–6.
- Those sections already teach past-participle forms, be + past participle, get + past participle, examples and transformation practice.
- Lesson 3 gives only a short reminder using its own cut/break/smash examples. It does not reteach passive/get-passive.

**Module 2 Lesson 9 — Ability, Obligation & Intention**

- Assumes basic sentence patterns, Operators and infinitive forms.
- Teaches the source's own can/ought/shall distinctions inside the Lesson:
  - can: ability / possibility;
  - ought: right/wise, duty/business, probability from facts;
  - shall in statements: will;
  - “Shall I ...?”: intention or whether an action is wise.
- unless → if ... not remains in conditional sentence structure, not this Lesson.

## 5. Cross-cutting material — not separate Modules

### 250 Fixed Word-Groups

- The original source has 250 items: 50 + 80 + 20 + 100.
- All 250 source items now have an instance-level meaning summary and provisional destination in BASIC_ENGLISH_250_FIXED_WORD_GROUPS_INSTANCE_INDEX.md.
- The separate A Basic Selection list of 50 Most Necessary groups is a priority subset selected from those 250, not 50 additional groups.
- Fixed groups are selected reinforcement inside relevant Lessons, not 250 flashcard-only Lessons or a separate Module.

### Words Formed by Endings

- The productive -er / -ing / -ed rules remain in the word-form foundation.
- Selected derived meanings are introduced where they naturally support a Lesson.
- A derived form's spelling/formation does not by itself prove its contextual meaning.

### Original 45 Steps

The original course already contains applied everyday scenes and integrated readings. The Expression System is a more explicit, systematic practice track; it is not presented as the first time Basic English is used to express real meanings.

## 6. Existing App prototype mapping

The five numbered lessons in the early App were **prototypes**, not five accepted curriculum Lessons. Their content is handled as follows:

| Early App prototype | What happens in the accepted curriculum |
|---|---|
| Lesson 01 — speak | Its teaching example is retained in **Module 2 · Lesson 1 (C01) — Communication & Information**. |
| Lesson 02 — body-part expansion | Its content is retained and expanded in **Module 1 · Lesson 1 (C13) — Names of Things: Meaning Expansion**. |
| Lesson 03 — mixed “Names of Acts” examples | It is not kept as one Lesson. The communication examples go to Module 2 · Lesson 1 (C01); touch/hit examples to Lesson 2 (C02); catch/run/movement examples to Lesson 4 (C04); think/understand/remember/forget examples to Lesson 5 (C05). |
| Lesson 04 — Fixed Word-Groups overview | It is not a standalone Lesson. Relevant fixed groups are used as reinforcement inside the Lessons where they support the meaning being taught. |
| Lesson 05 — Some Common Needs overview | It is not a standalone Lesson. Its material is redistributed by meaning: can/ought to Module 2 · Lesson 9 (C09); unless to the conditional-sentence foundation; busy/deaf/home/world to Module 2 · Lesson 12 (C12). The source's “finish” sentence is explanatory English, not a Basic rendering, so it is not taught as a replacement. |

Do not count these five early prototypes as five extra Lessons. The current accepted curriculum has 15 Lessons in total. Do not renumber or move App screens until a separate migration plan is prepared and approved by the project's implementation workflow.
