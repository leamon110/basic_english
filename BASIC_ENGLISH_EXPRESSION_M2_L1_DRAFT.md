# Module 2 · Lesson 1 — Communication & Information

> **Draft status:** first integrated teaching-content draft for the accepted curriculum's C01. It reuses the existing speak prototype instead of creating a second copy. The original Basic expressions below are from Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English*, pp.244–245; the short contexts, explanations, exercises and integration notes are project-created. The compact **show** comparison is from p.252. Clarity, exercise volume and actual learner time still require review.

## What this lesson teaches

When people communicate, information can be expressed, delivered, received, read or sent. The useful question is not “Which Basic word replaces this English verb?” It is:

**What is happening to the information, who is involved, and how does it move?**

This lesson keeps three connected parts together:

1. expressing or delivering information;
2. receiving and processing information;
3. a short comparison showing how the word **show** changes meaning in different contexts.

The first two parts are one lesson because they describe the two sides of the same information event. They are not separate lessons or a list of unrelated verbs.

## 2. Expressing and delivering information

The source examples can be understood through the role of the information and what the speaker does with it.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is being expressed |
|---|---|---|
| tell a story | give it | The story is the content being given |
| give an account / tell what happened | give an account; give the news | Information about an event is delivered |
| tell someone how to do something | give directions | The person receives instructions about what to do |
| “as I was telling you” | “as I was saying” | The speaker refers back to what was being said |
| sing | give a song | The song is treated as something given to listeners |
| write one's views | put one's views into writing / down on paper | The speaker's views are put into written form |
| send a letter to a newspaper | send a letter to the paper | A written message is sent to its destination |

A few distinctions matter:

- **give it** refers to the story or account already mentioned; it is not a free-standing replacement for every use of tell.
- **give directions** is for telling someone how to do something. It is not the same meaning as telling a story.
- **put one's views into writing** describes turning views into written form. It does not mean sending them to someone; sending is a further action with a destination.
- The source's **send a letter to the paper** remains a direct Basic expression. Do not rewrite it just to avoid using send.

## 3. Receiving and processing information

The source distinguishes information reaching a person from the person's active attention to it.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| hear music | the music comes to the ears of those who hear it | Sound reaches the listener |
| listen to music | give it their attention | The listener actively attends to the sound |
| hear an account later | have news of it | The person learns the information afterwards |
| read something in a newspaper | see it in the paper / be reading it | The information is encountered in written form |
| read something carefully | go through it with care | The reader examines the information carefully |

Do not collapse these differences:

- **hear** describes sound reaching the ears;
- **listen** adds attention from the person receiving the sound;
- **have news of it** describes later knowledge of an event or account;
- **see it in the paper** and **go through it with care** do not describe the same depth of reading.

## 4. A short comparison: the different meanings of show

The source returns to **show** on p.252. These examples are included as a short comparison because one ordinary word can refer to different events. They are not all communication acts.

| What “show” means in context | Basic expression from the source | Topic connection |
|---|---|---|
| show the effect of the past on the present | make it clear | Making an idea or relation clear; cross-link to knowledge / understanding |
| show pictures | let someone see them / put them on view | Display |
| show old buildings to visitors | take them round | Guiding people to see places |
| show interest | give signs of it | Showing an attitude; cross-link to feeling / attitude |
| try to show interest that is not there | make an attempt to give signs of an interest which is not there | The attempt pattern; supporting example only |

The lesson's purpose here is to prevent a false rule such as **show = let someone see**. That expression fits pictures, but not every source use of show.

Keep this comparison compact. The meanings of clear understanding and interest belong more fully with their related topics; they are not expanded into separate C01 sections.

## 5. How to choose the expression

When you meet a communication verb, ask:

1. **What is the event?** Is someone expressing, delivering, receiving, reading, recording or sending information?
2. **Who is involved?** Who provides the information, and who receives it?
3. **What is the content?** A story, account, directions, song, view, letter or other information?
4. **What is the route or medium?** Spoken words, sound, writing, a paper, or a destination?
5. **Is the ordinary word being used in another sense?** For example, speak a language is about knowledge, not a speaking event.

Do not choose an Operator or a fixed phrase before identifying the event. The source examples use several different Basic structures; they are not one formula.

## Practice

### A. Identify the event before choosing the expression

1. Someone tells another person how to do a task. Which source expression fits?
   - A. give a song
   - B. give directions
   - C. have news of it

2. Someone tells a story. Which source expression fits?
   - A. give it
   - B. give directions
   - C. put it on view

3. Someone hears music without deliberately attending to it. Which description fits?
   - A. give it their attention
   - B. the music comes to the ears
   - C. go through it with care

4. Someone listens carefully to music. Which description adds the listener's active role?
   - A. give it their attention
   - B. have news of it
   - C. send a letter to the paper

5. Someone learns about an account later. Which expression fits?
   - A. put views into writing
   - B. have news of it
   - C. give a talk

**Answers and reasons**

1. B — Directions tell the receiver how to do something.
2. A — The source gives tell a story → give it; it refers to the story.
3. B — The source describes heard music as coming to the ears.
4. A — Listening adds attention from the receiver.
5. B — The source gives have news of it for hearing an account later.

### B. Distinguish writing, reading and sending

Choose the expression that matches each event.

1. A person puts their views into written form.
2. A person reads an account carefully.
3. A person sends a letter to a newspaper.

**Suggested answers**

1. put one's views into writing / down on paper;
2. go through it with care;
3. send a letter to the paper.

These are different actions: recording information, examining written information, and sending a written message to a destination.

### C. Keep the speak boundary clear

Classify each meaning:

1. speak to a person;
2. speak at a meeting;
3. speak a language.

**Answers**

1. Communication: say something to him.
2. Communication: give a talk / make a statement.
3. Knowledge of a language: have a knowledge of it. Cross-link this use to the knowledge topic; do not classify it as an ordinary speaking event.

This reuses the existing speak prototype. It is not a second copy of that prototype's full quiz or writing exercise.

### D. Distinguish the meanings of show

Match each context with the source expression.

1. A teacher makes the effect of the past on the present understandable.
2. A person displays pictures.
3. A guide takes visitors to see old buildings.
4. A person gives signs of interest.

**Suggested answers**

1. make it clear;
2. let someone see them / put them on view;
3. take them round;
4. give signs of it.

The same English word does not carry the same event in all four cases.

### E. Reverse understanding

Explain the event described by each Basic expression:

1. give a song;
2. give directions;
3. give it their attention;
4. have news of it;
5. put one's views down on paper;
6. go through it with care.

**Suggested answers**

1. Sing; the song is treated as something given.
2. Tell someone how to do something.
3. Listen with attention.
4. Learn information about an event or account later.
5. Write one's views.
6. Read or examine something carefully.

### F. Independent construction

Choose one new context of your own for each group:

- delivering information;
- receiving information;
- writing or sending information.

Write the intended meaning first, then choose a source-supported Basic expression and explain why it fits. Compare your reasoning with the role/content/receiver/medium questions in Section 5.

## Lesson review

You should now be able to:

- identify the communication event before choosing a Basic expression;
- distinguish expressing/delivering information from receiving or processing it;
- distinguish hearing from listening, and ordinary reading from careful reading;
- express writing and sending as different actions;
- recognize that one word such as show can describe different events;
- keep speak a language as a knowledge boundary example rather than misclassifying it as communication.
