# Module 2 · Lesson 12 — Context-Dependent Words & Paraphrase

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft, based on *The ABC of Basic English*, printed pp.260–262, and the source-checked C12 teaching flow. It covers the seven source examples and preserves their different contexts. Closed exercises have answer keys. Actual learner time and feedback are unmeasured; this draft is not user-tested or App-integrated.

## What this lesson teaches

Some ordinary-English words are not entries in the Basic 850. The learner must still be able to express their meanings.

The task is not to find one replacement word and memorize it. It is to:

1. identify what the ordinary-English word refers to in this sentence;
2. choose the Basic description that preserves that meaning;
3. check that the description still refers to the same person, thing, place or condition.

This lesson uses seven examples from the source: busy, deaf, world, life, hill, home and address. Each needs context. The lesson does not claim that these seven examples provide a complete method for every word outside the Basic list.

## 1. busy and deaf — describe the condition

### busy

The source first gives a description built around the Name business. It then says that a more natural description is preferable.

Choose according to the intended meaning:

- If the sentence emphasizes that the person has many tasks waiting, the Basic description is **has much to do**.
- If it emphasizes the person's active effort, use a description of the work being done, rather than treating busy as the name of a job or profession.

The source's first description is not the preferred everyday form. Do not teach busy as meaning that someone is a business person.

### deaf

Describe the person's hearing condition. A simple learner-built paraphrase is **poor hearing**.

This does not say that the person cannot speak, cannot understand, or has any other condition. Add only what the sentence supports.

### What to notice

Busy and deaf are ordinary-English Quality words outside the Basic list. The task is to describe the condition with Basic words. This is not the Module 1 lesson on extending the meaning of an already-known Basic Quality.

## 2. world — identify what the word refers to

The source gives three different readings.

| Context | Meaning to identify | Basic description |
|---|---|---|
| A statement about the physical world or space | The planet itself | earth |
| A statement about the people of the world as one group | All the people being considered | every one |
| A statement about the state of society or what is changing | The state of society | things or conditions |

The same ordinary-English word does not have one fixed Basic replacement. The sentence decides which of the three readings in the table is intended.

## 3. life — an abstract sense, living objects or the activity of living

The source distinguishes three uses:

1. **Abstract meaning:** life can refer to the fact that something exists. The Basic meaning is **existence**.
2. **Objects that have life:** when the sentence refers to people, animals or plants as living objects, use **living things** or all of these objects.
3. **The activity itself:** the source also shows the -ing form used by itself as a Name for the activity of living.

Keep these apart. A sentence about the abstract sense of life is not necessarily talking about the living objects themselves. A sentence about the activity is different again.

The third use cross-links to the existing -ing word-form foundation. It does not make the -ing form a universal replacement for every use of life.

## 4. hill — one raised landform or a region

A hill is a piece of land that stands higher than the land around it.

The source gives **a slope** as one short description. It also allows a comparison with a small mountain when the size fits the particular landform.

Do not force the mountain comparison onto every hill. The scale and shape matter.

The source also discusses a whole region whose land contains many hills. That is not one hill. Describe the region itself, for example as an area or country with many hills.

**850-word boundary:** the source mentions highlands for this regional context, but that term is absent from the project's 850-word list. Do not present it as a Basic word. Use a description built from Basic words instead.

## 5. home — residence or family state

### The place where a person lives

Home refers to the place where the person actually lives. It does not require the person to own a house or to live in a separate building.

A short stay in a hotel does not, by itself, make that hotel the person's home.

### The family-state context

The source also gives a context in which the intended meaning is **with his family**.

This is a particular reading about the person's family situation. It is not a universal replacement for home. When the sentence is about the place of residence, keep the place meaning.

## 6. address — identify which address is meant

The source gives three contexts:

| Context | What the ordinary-English word means | Basic description |
|---|---|---|
| Private address | Where the person lives | The place where the person lives |
| Business address | The location connected with the person's business | place of business |
| Address written at the top of a letter | The heading of the letter | letter-heading |

Do not assume that address always means a person's home. A private address, a business address and the heading written on a letter are different things.

**Scope:** this lesson covers address as a Name for a location or a letter heading. It does not add the verb use of address someone.

## 7. The context-first paraphrase method

Use the same short method for every target word:

1. **Find the target.** Which ordinary-English word needs a Basic description?
2. **Read the whole context.** What person, thing, place, condition or activity is being referred to?
3. **Choose the sense.** Which of the source's meanings fits this sentence?
4. **Build the description.** Use Basic words and the word forms already taught.
5. **Read it back.** Does the Basic description preserve the same meaning?
6. **Check uncertainty.** If two meanings still fit, do not guess. Ask for more context or keep the broader description.

The method is the same, but the actual Basic expression changes with the meaning.

## 8. Keep this lesson separate from the other curriculum topics

| Curriculum topic | Starting point | Learner's task |
|---|---|---|
| Module 2 · Lesson 12 (this lesson) | An ordinary-English word outside the Basic list | Identify its contextual meaning and express it with Basic words |
| Module 1 · Lesson 3 | A word already in the Basic list | Use context to identify a narrower referent within that word's meaning |
| Module 1 · Lessons 1–2 | A word already in the Basic list | Understand source-recognized related meaning expansion or use as a Thing |

The direction of the task matters. C12 is not a second meaning-expansion lesson and is not a second Special Use / narrowing lesson.

Other boundaries:

- **unless → if ... not** remains in the conditional-sentence foundation. It is not added to this Lesson.
- **finish** is not a Basic replacement and is not added to this Lesson.
- busy and deaf are not taught as Names of Qualities expansion.
- The source's seven examples are the scope of this Lesson's source-based teaching. Do not imply that the current App can automatically paraphrase every word outside the 850.

## Practice

### A. busy and deaf

Choose the description that preserves the intended meaning.

1. A sentence says that a person is busy because many tasks still need attention. Which kind of description fits?
   - A. A description of the amount of work the person has to do.
   - B. A description of the person's business as a profession.
   - C. A description of the person's hearing.

2. A sentence emphasizes that a person is actively putting effort into work. Which kind of description fits?
   - A. A description of the person's hearing.
   - B. A description of the work the person is doing.
   - C. A description of the person's home.

3. A person is described as deaf. Which condition must the paraphrase describe?
   - A. Hearing.
   - B. Speaking.
   - C. Movement.

4. Are busy and deaf entries in the project's Basic 850 list?
   - A. Yes. They are two Basic Qualities.
   - B. No. They are ordinary-English target words that this lesson describes with Basic words.

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The context emphasizes the amount of work waiting to be done.
2. B — The context emphasizes the person's activity, not the number of tasks.
3. A — The source's description is about hearing. It does not state that the person cannot speak.
4. B — Both target words are absent from the current 850 list.

### B. world and life

5. A sentence uses world to refer to the physical planet. Which reading is intended?
   - A. The people considered as one group.
   - B. The physical planet.
   - C. The changing state of society.

6. A sentence uses world to refer to all the people considered together. Which reading is intended?
   - A. The physical planet.
   - B. A private address.
   - C. The collective-people reading.

7. A sentence says that the world is changing, and the context concerns the state of society. Which description fits?
   - A. The changing state of society.
   - B. A person's home.
   - C. A single raised landform.

8. A sentence uses life to mean the fact that something exists. Which sense is intended?
   - A. The abstract sense.
   - B. The group of living objects.
   - C. The activity of learning.

9. A sentence uses life to refer to people, animals and plants as objects that have life. Which sense is intended?
   - A. A private location.
   - B. Living objects.
   - C. The physical planet.

10. The source uses an -ing form by itself as a Name for an activity. Which foundation does this example connect to?
   - A. The word-form foundation for -ing.
   - B. The number foundation.
   - C. The conditional-sentence foundation.

**Answers and reasons**

5. B — The context is physical space, not people or social conditions.
6. C — The word refers to the people as a group.
7. A — The sentence concerns changing circumstances, not the planet itself.
8. A — The sentence is about the abstract meaning of life.
9. B — The reference is to living entities, not an abstract idea.
10. A — The source's example uses the -ing form as a Name. It cross-links to the existing word-form foundation.

### C. hill, home and address

11. A sentence describes one raised piece of land. Which short description from the source fits?
   - A. The slope description.
   - B. The business-location description.
   - C. The family-state description.

12. Can every hill be described as a mountain?
   - A. Yes. Size never matters.
   - B. No. The comparison is used only when the size fits.
   - C. No. A hill always means a whole region.

13. A sentence refers to a whole region containing many hills, not one raised piece of land. What should the learner describe?
   - A. The region itself.
   - B. One person's private address.
   - C. One person's hearing.

14. Does home always mean a house owned by the person?
   - A. Yes.
   - B. No. It refers to where the person lives and does not require ownership of a house.

15. A person stays in a hotel for one night. Does that fact alone make the hotel the person's home?
   - A. Yes.
   - B. No. A short stay alone does not establish that it is the person's home.

16. In the source's family-state context, what is being described?
   - A. The person's family situation.
   - B. The address written on a letter.
   - C. A region of hills.

17. A form asks for a person's private address. Which information is required?
   - A. The place where the person lives.
   - B. The location of the person's business.
   - C. The heading at the top of a letter.

18. A business letter has an address for the business and another heading written at the top of the letter. Are these the same thing?
   - A. Yes. Both always mean the person's home.
   - B. No. One identifies the business location; the other is the letter's heading.

**Answers and reasons**

11. A — The question is about one raised landform.
12. B — The source makes the comparison depend on size.
13. A — The referent is the region, not one hill.
14. B — A person's home is where the person lives; ownership is not the test.
15. B — A temporary hotel stay does not by itself establish a home.
16. A — This is the family-state context, not the place/address meaning.
17. A — A private address identifies where the person lives.
18. B — The business location and the heading written on a letter serve different purposes.

### D. Keep C12 and neighbouring curriculum topics separate

19. A learner starts with an ordinary-English word outside the Basic list and must describe its meaning with Basic words. Which curriculum topic is this?

**Answers and reasons**

### E. Reverse understanding

For each Basic description, identify the source word and the context that it can represent. Do not give only the word; explain which sense is intended.

1. The physical planet considered as a place in space.
2. People considered together as one group.
3. The changing state of society or circumstances.
4. The abstract sense of life.
5. People, animals or plants considered as entities that have life.
6. An -ing form used by itself as a Name for an activity.
7. A raised piece of land, described by its sloping form.
8. A whole region whose land contains many hills.
9. The actual place where a person lives.
10. A family-state context rather than a location.
11. The location identified for a person's private purposes.
12. The location connected with a business.
13. The heading written at the top of a letter.
14. A person with many tasks waiting to be done.
15. A person whose hearing is poor.

**Suggested answers**

1. world — physical/planet sense.
2. world — people-as-a-group sense.
3. world — social conditions / changing circumstances.
4. life — the abstract sense.
5. life — living-object sense.
6. life — the activity sense, using the -ing word form.
7. hill — one raised landform.
8. hill / the regional hills context — describe the region, not one hill; do not use the source's regional term as a Basic entry.
9. home — residence sense.
10. home — family-state sense.
11. address — private address.
12. address — business address.
13. address — the heading written at the top of a letter.
14. busy — amount-of-work sense.
15. deaf — hearing condition.

### F. Independent construction

Complete all four tasks. First identify the intended sense, then construct a Basic description, and finally explain why another sense does not fit.

1. Write two short contexts for world: one about the physical planet and one about people as a group. Give a different Basic description for each.
2. Write two contexts for life: one about its abstract sense and one about living objects. Explain why the same description cannot be used automatically in both.
3. Describe a hill as one landform, then describe a whole region containing many hills. Keep the two referents separate.
4. Write three contexts for address: private, business and the heading on a letter. Give a separate Basic description for each.

**Checking boundary:** these independent responses are not automatically scored by the current App. Do not claim that the current 850-word checker or the existing sentence checker evaluates arbitrary C12 paraphrases until a separate rule set has been designed and tested.
