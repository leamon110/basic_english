# Basic English Expression System — Candidate Module / Lesson Structure v0.1

> **Status: provisional design for stress-testing, not final acceptance.**
>
> This structure is the first result after auditing the major source groups by learner decision problem. It deliberately does not inherit the source's Simple Acts / Complex Acts / Other Suggestions headings as Lesson boundaries.

## Module 1 — From ordinary meaning to a Basic expression

**Core learning operation:**  
ordinary English meaning → identify the actual intended relation/action → construct a Basic expression → reverse-check the meaning.

| Candidate Lesson | Scope | Why it belongs together | Main source material |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Communication & Information | Different communication verbs require different Basic reconstructions depending on what is communicated and how | speak, tell, sing, hear, listen, write, read, send |
| 02 | Bodily Action & Contact | Physical bodily actions can be expressed by Operators plus body/object relations; the learner must identify the actual physical action | eat, bite, spit, touch, hit, scratch |
| 03 | Physical Change & Result | The central problem becomes change of form, damage and resulting state | cut, break, smash, destroy, hurt |
| 04 | Movement, Transfer & Position | Identify movement, source/destination, viewpoint, and body/object position | throw, catch, hold, bring, lie, stand, sit, run, slip |
| 05 | Thinking, Knowledge, Meaning & Memory | Mental verbs are reconstructed as explicit mental states/relations | think, know, understand, mean, remember, forget |
| 06 | Feeling, Preference, Desire & Need | Internal experience and attitude are expressed as relations to objects/people | feel, like, want, need |
| 07 | Learning, Teaching, Showing & Attempting | Acquisition/transfer of knowledge plus the semantic variations of show and the “make an attempt” pattern | learn, teach, show, try |
| 08 | Social Exchange, Relations & Participation | Exchange, commitment, interpersonal relation and participation share a social-relation frame | buy, sell, promise, take part in, one another, related fixed groups |
| 09 | State, Growth, Life & Process | The learner expresses changes of state, life processes and process boundaries | grow, sleep, die, begin, finish |
| 10 | Modality & Condition | Express possibility, ability, duty, advisability and conditional meaning | can, ought, shall, unless; be able to etc. |
| 11 | Quantity, Degree & Time | Express amount, distribution, degree and temporal position/sequence | each, altogether, whole, few, many, too, once; ago, already, yet, since, next, always |

### Module 1 cross-links

The following are **not forced into separate Lessons** merely because they occur in the source:

- find / lose → mainly knowledge/location/movement contexts;
- destroy / hurt → physical change/result;
- show → meaning-dependent, with communication and learning cross-links;
- run → movement/position, but also a useful meaning-contrast example;
- try → supporting “make an attempt” pattern;
- busy / deaf → quality paraphrase and/or meaning-expansion material;
- world / life / hill / home / address → contextual meaning material.

## Module 2 — Understanding the meanings Basic words can carry

**Core learning operation:**  
known Basic word → identify the root sense → identify the relation producing the new sense → determine whether the use is expansion or specialization → reverse-check.

| Candidate Lesson | Scope | Why it belongs together | Main source material |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Expansion of Names of Things | Related objects/situations are understood through part-whole, sensory, use/behavior and other relations | Names of Things expansions |
| 13 | Expansion of Names of Qualities | Quality words extend through human reaction and associated properties | Names of Qualities expansions |
| 14 | Special Use & Contextual Meaning | Context narrows a general word; quality/thing boundaries and special senses require contextual interpretation | special use / specialization; quality↔thing cases; contextual common-name examples |

## Why there are only two candidate Modules

### Not a separate Fixed-Word-Group Module

The 250 Fixed Word-Groups and the 50 Most Necessary groups occur across:

- movement;
- state;
- attitude;
- modality;
- time;
- social relations;
- process;
- memory;
- causation;
- etc.

They are therefore a **reinforcement layer**, not a coherent learning task of their own.

### Not a separate Endings Module inside this Expression course

The -er / -ing / -ed material is a reusable word-form mechanism.

Its examples appear in physical action, communication, learning, states and many other topics. The productive rule belongs to the basic word-form foundation; important derived meanings should be reinforced where they become useful.

### Not a separate Operator/Directive Module

Operators, Directives, word forms and sentence structure are foundational tools already handled by the Basic English teaching/45-Step side of the project.

The Expression System should **use those tools to solve meaning problems**, not duplicate the foundation.

## Current candidate count

**2 candidate Modules / 14 candidate Lessons.**

This number is **not final**.

The 14 are the smallest current set that gives each clearly different learning task a visible home without turning every source subsection or individual word into a Lesson.

## Stress points that must still be tested

### Stress point 1 — Lesson 02

“eat/bite/spit” and “touch/hit/scratch” are related physical actions but not identical. The candidate combines them because both train the learner to describe bodily contact/action using Basic components. This needs a lesson-size test.

### Stress point 2 — Lesson 04

Object transfer and body posture are different decision problems. The candidate combines them because both are movement/position material and strongly reuse the same source/destination/location tools. This is a deliberate compression that must be tested.

### Stress point 3 — Lesson 07

Learning/teaching is coherent; show is polysemous; try is currently a supporting pattern. This Lesson may need restructuring after practice design.

### Stress point 4 — Lesson 08

The direct Some Common Needs source material is relatively small. It needs the fixed-word-group and participation/relationship material to be tested before being accepted as a standalone Lesson.

### Stress point 5 — Lesson 10 / 11

Modality, condition, quantity, degree and time are all function-oriented expressions but do not share exactly the same semantic task. The current grouping is only a candidate compression and must be tested.

### Stress point 6 — Module 2

The boundary between:

- expansion;
- specialization;
- quality↔thing;
- contextual special sense

may support 2 Lessons instead of 3, or require a different division. This needs actual exercise design before final counting.

## Acceptance rule for v0.1

The candidate structure can become the final structure only if every Lesson passes all of these tests:

1. It has one dominant learner decision problem.
2. Its source examples form enough material for meaningful practice.
3. It does not duplicate a foundation lesson.
4. Fixed groups and endings can be inserted naturally without distorting the Lesson.
5. A learner can move from meaning → Basic construction → reverse interpretation.
6. Adjacent Lessons have a visible reason for being separate.
7. No Lesson exists merely because the source has a heading or because a target count needs another item.

Until those tests are passed, **14 is a candidate count, not a project commitment.**
