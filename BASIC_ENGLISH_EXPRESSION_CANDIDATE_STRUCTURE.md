# Basic English Expression System — Candidate Module / Lesson Structure v0.1

> **Status: v0.1 has failed its first structural stress test; it is retained as a record of the initial candidate, not an accepted syllabus. See “Stress Test 01” below.**
>
> This structure is the first result after auditing the major source groups by learner decision problem. It deliberately does not inherit the source's Simple Acts / Complex Acts / Other Suggestions headings as Lesson boundaries.

## Module 1 — From ordinary meaning to a Basic expression

**Core learning operation:**  
ordinary English meaning → identify the actual intended relation/action → construct a Basic expression → reverse-check the meaning.

| Candidate Lesson | Scope | Why it belongs together | Main source material |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Communication & Information | Different communication verbs require different Basic reconstructions depending on what is communicated and how | speak, tell, sing, hear, listen, write, read, send |
| 02 | Bodily Action & Contact | Physical bodily actions can be expressed by Operators plus body/object relations; the learner must identify the actual physical action | eat, bite, spit, touch, hit, scratch |
| 03 | Physical Change & Result | The central problem becomes change of form, damage and resulting state | cut, break, smash, destroy, hurt |
| 04 | Movement, Transfer & Position | Identify movement, source/destination, viewpoint, and body/object position | throw, catch, hold, bring, lie, stand, sit, run, slip |
| 05 | Thinking, Knowledge, Meaning & Memory | Mental verbs are reconstructed as explicit mental states/relations | think, know, understand, mean, remember, forget |
| 06 | Feeling, Preference, Desire & Need | Internal experience and attitude are expressed as relations to objects/people | feel, like, want, need |
| 07 | Learning, Teaching, Showing & Attempting | Acquisition/transfer of knowledge plus the semantic variations of show and the “make an attempt” pattern | learn, teach, show, try |
| 08 | Social Exchange, Relations & Participation | Exchange, commitment, interpersonal relation and participation share a social-relation frame | buy, sell, promise, take part in, one another, related fixed groups |
| 09 | State, Growth, Life & Process | The learner expresses changes of state, life processes and process boundaries | grow, sleep, die, begin, finish |
| 10 | Modality & Condition | Express possibility, ability, duty, advisability and conditional meaning | can, ought, shall, unless; be able to etc. |
| 11 | Quantity, Degree & Time | Express amount, distribution, degree and temporal position/sequence | each, altogether, whole, few, many, too, once; ago, already, yet, since, next, always |

### Module 1 cross-links

The following are **not forced into separate Lessons** merely because they occur in the source:

- find / lose → mainly knowledge/location/movement contexts;
- destroy / hurt → physical change/result;
- show → meaning-dependent, with communication and learning cross-links;
- run → movement/position, but also a useful meaning-contrast example;
- try → supporting “make an attempt” pattern;
- busy / deaf → quality paraphrase and/or meaning-expansion material;
- world / life / hill / home / address → contextual meaning material.

## Module 2 — Understanding the meanings Basic words can carry

**Core learning operation:**  
known Basic word → identify the root sense → identify the relation producing the new sense → determine whether the use is expansion or specialization → reverse-check.

| Candidate Lesson | Scope | Why it belongs together | Main source material |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Expansion of Names of Things | Related objects/situations are understood through part-whole, sensory, use/behavior and other relations | Names of Things expansions |
| 13 | Expansion of Names of Qualities | Quality words extend through human reaction and associated properties | Names of Qualities expansions |
| 14 | Special Use & Contextual Meaning | Context narrows a general word; quality/thing boundaries and special senses require contextual interpretation | special use / specialization; quality↔thing cases; contextual common-name examples |

## Why there are only two candidate Modules

### Not a separate Fixed-Word-Group Module

The 250 Fixed Word-Groups and the 50 Most Necessary groups occur across:

- movement;
- state;
- attitude;
- modality;
- time;
- social relations;
- process;
- memory;
- causation;
- etc.

They are therefore a **reinforcement layer**, not a coherent learning task of their own.

### Not a separate Endings Module inside this Expression course

The -er / -ing / -ed material is a reusable word-form mechanism.

Its examples appear in physical action, communication, learning, states and many other topics. The productive rule belongs to the basic word-form foundation; important derived meanings should be reinforced where they become useful.

### Not a separate Operator/Directive Module

Operators, Directives, word forms and sentence structure are foundational tools already handled by the Basic English teaching/45-Step side of the project.

The Expression System should **use those tools to solve meaning problems**, not duplicate the foundation.

## Current candidate count

**2 candidate Modules / 14 candidate Lessons.**

This number is **not final**.

The 14 are the smallest current set that gives each clearly different learning task a visible home without turning every source subsection or individual word into a Lesson.

## Stress points that must still be tested

### Stress point 1 — Lesson 02

“eat/bite/spit” and “touch/hit/scratch” are related physical actions but not identical. The candidate combines them because both train the learner to describe bodily contact/action using Basic components. This needs a lesson-size test.

### Stress point 2 — Lesson 04

Object transfer and body posture are different decision problems. The candidate combines them because both are movement/position material and strongly reuse the same source/destination/location tools. This is a deliberate compression that must be tested.

### Stress point 3 — Lesson 07

Learning/teaching is coherent; show is polysemous; try is currently a supporting pattern. This Lesson may need restructuring after practice design.

### Stress point 4 — Lesson 08

The direct Some Common Needs source material is relatively small. It needs the fixed-word-group and participation/relationship material to be tested before being accepted as a standalone Lesson.

### Stress point 5 — Lesson 10 / 11

Modality, condition, quantity, degree and time are all function-oriented expressions but do not share exactly the same semantic task. The current grouping is only a candidate compression and must be tested.

### Stress point 6 — Module 2

The boundary between:

- expansion;
- specialization;
- quality↔thing;
- contextual special sense

may support 2 Lessons instead of 3, or require a different division. This needs actual exercise design before final counting.

## Acceptance rule for v0.1

The candidate structure can become the final structure only if every Lesson passes all of these tests:

1. It has one dominant learner decision problem.
2. Its source examples form enough material for meaningful practice.
3. It does not duplicate a foundation lesson.
4. Fixed groups and endings can be inserted naturally without distorting the Lesson.
5. A learner can move from meaning → Basic construction → reverse interpretation.
6. Adjacent Lessons have a visible reason for being separate.
7. No Lesson exists merely because the source has a heading or because a target count needs another item.

Until those tests are passed, **14 is a candidate count, not a project commitment.**

### 2. Important source-classification corrections

These corrections matter more than the current Lesson count:

1. **“Busy” and “deaf” are Other Suggestions expression examples.** Their task is to describe an ordinary-English Quality with Basic words (e.g. “working hard / has much to do”; “whose hearing is bad”). They are not Names of Qualities meaning-expansion examples.
2. **“World”, “life”, “hill”, “home” and “address” are also Other Suggestions.** They require context-sensitive expression decisions. They must not automatically be placed in the Names of Things special-use/narrowing lesson.
3. **Qualities used as Names of Things** is a word-class/function boundary. It is not the same as Special Use / narrowing. Link it to the existing word-class foundation and include it in a semantic lesson only if the source examples require it.
4. **“Unless”** is the conditional pattern “if ... not”; it should not be grouped with modal meanings merely because both occur in Other Suggestions.
5. **The 50 priority fixed groups are reinforcement material, not a reason to create a “social relations” lesson.** For example, “take part in”, “one another”, “respect for”, “dependent on” and “let a house” have different natural uses. Insert each where its meaning belongs.
6. The “special use” discussed in Names of Things and Qualities (meaning narrowing) must not be confused with the 250 Fixed Word-Groups section's special/idiomatic uses.

### 3. Source-index correction made during this audit

The project index had duplicated the “a plate breaks itself → it comes to bits” example: once under physical change in section 2.3 and again under movement/posture in section 2.4. The second, misplaced row has been removed. The example remains once under physical change. The movement/posture group therefore has **14 unique indexed rows**, not 15.

### 4. What currently survives the stress test

## Teaching-size test 01 — Bodily action/contact vs physical effects/result

The purpose of this test is to see whether candidate 02 and 03 can be taught as two short, natural units with different decisions, rather than merely splitting a source paragraph.

### Candidate 02 — Bodily actions and contact

**Teaching objective:** identify what bodily action is happening and what relation between the body, the object and the action must be made explicit.

Two internal sections are needed:

**A. Intake, biting and expulsion**

- eat → take food / have a meal
- bite a piece → have a bite of it / at it
- spit food out → put it out of your mouth

**B. Contact, impact and rubbing**

- touch something → put a hand/finger on it / give it a touch
- touch by chance → come up against it
- hit something hard → give it a blow
- scratch with a pointed object → make a mark on it
- scratch an insect bite → give it a rub
- scratch deeply → get one's nails into it

**Project-created test prompts based on the source examples:**

1. Someone is eating a meal. Choose between “take food” and “have a meal” according to what the sentence emphasizes.
2. Someone bites a piece off an apple. Express the action with “have a bite”.
3. Someone spits food out. Preserve the outward direction and body location: “put it out of your mouth”.
4. Someone touches a wall to test whether it is dry. Express the body part and contact relation: “put a hand on it”.
5. Someone hits an object hard. Express the impact as “give it a blow”.
6. Someone scratches an insect bite to reduce irritation. Do not use “make a mark”; the source gives “give it a rub”.
7. Someone scratches a surface with a pointed object. Here the relevant result is “make a mark”.

**Test result:** this can be taught as one compact bodily-action theme if the two sections remain explicit. The common task is identifying the actual bodily event and the body/object relation; it is **not** a single reusable grammar formula. It is therefore retained as one candidate Lesson for now, but it must not be described as one uniform construction.

### Candidate 03 — Physical effects: damage, change and result

**Teaching objective:** distinguish an action that changes or damages something from the resulting state, and from a change that happens by itself.

**Source-grounded contrasts:**

- cut a person → give the person a cut;
- cut through thread or cake → get it cut;
- break a thread → the thread is broken / get it broken;
- smash a plate → the plate may be smashed;
- a plate breaks by itself → it comes to bits;
- destroy something → burn it / smash it / break it / put an end to it;
- hurt someone → give them pain / give wounds / do serious damage.

**Project-created test prompts based on the source examples:**

1. A person cuts another person. Express the injury event, not the physical route of a cutting tool.
2. A thread is cut through. Distinguish the affected object and the result construction.
3. A thread is already broken. Describe the resulting state rather than the action that caused it.
4. A plate is smashed by someone. Keep the agent-caused result distinct from the plate breaking by itself.
5. A plate breaks by itself. Use “come to bits”, not the agentive “give it a blow”.
6. Someone hurts another person. Choose whether the context concerns pain, wounds or serious damage.

**Test result:** this is a genuinely different teaching task from candidate 02. The learner must distinguish action, caused result, resulting state and self-occurring change. The source itself marks cutting/breaking as operations connected with changes of form. Keep this as a separate candidate Lesson and rename it **Physical Effects: Damage, Change & Result**.

### Boundary decision after Teaching-size test 01

- Candidate 02 remains one provisional Lesson with two explicit subtopics: intake/expulsion and contact/force.
- Candidate 03 remains a separate Lesson because it teaches action/result/state distinctions that candidate 02 does not.
- The boundary is based on the learner's decision, not the source's page break.
- This test does not yet establish the final total Lesson count.


## Teaching-size test 02 — Movement, transfer and body position

### Candidate 04 — Movement and Position: Transfer, Posture & Viewpoint

**Teaching objective:** describe where a person or thing is, where it moves, and how the movement/position relates to a reference point. The learner must distinguish transfer/path, held position, posture change and the speaker's viewpoint.

Two internal sections are needed.

**A. Moving, sending and holding an object**

Source examples:

- throw a ball to a baby → send the ball to a baby;
- throw over a wall / across a river → send the ball over / across;
- a blow pushes someone against a table → the blow sends the person against the table;
- catch a ball → get it in the hand;
- hold a ball → get a grip of it / keep it / have it in the hand;
- bring delicate things → take them / come with them, depending on viewpoint.

The central decision is: **what moves, what is the destination/path, and from whose viewpoint is the movement described?**

**B. Position and change of posture**

Source examples:

- lie on something → be on it / rest on it;
- stand up → get up;
- sit → take a seat / be seated.

The central decision is: **is the sentence describing an existing position, or the action of moving into that position?**

**Supporting meaning contrasts**

- run quickly → go quickly;
- run for health → take a run;
- run away from danger → go (off) at a run;
- slip → have a slip / have a fall.

These examples are useful because they show that one ordinary word can describe speed, purposeful activity or escape, and that an accidental event can be expressed as a Name. They do not need to become independent Lessons.

**Additional source examples for path practice**

- take your arm out of your coat;
- put money into your pocket;
- go quickly out of the house.

**Project-created test prompts based on the source examples:**

1. A ball is thrown across a river. Preserve the path: “send the ball across the river”.
2. A child catches a ball. Express the new position: “get it in the hand”.
3. A person keeps a ball in their hand. Compare “keep it”, “have it in the hand” and “get a grip of it”; do not treat them as identical in every context.
4. A person is bringing eggs toward the speaker. Decide between “take them” and “come with them” from the speaker's viewpoint.
5. A person is already sitting. Use the state expression “be seated”; when telling someone to sit, use the action expression “take a seat”.
6. A person stands up from lying down. Express the change of posture: “get up”.
7. Compare “run quickly”, “run for health” and “run away from danger”. Do not give all three the same Basic rendering.
8. Someone slips and falls. Express the event with “have a slip / have a fall”.

### Test result

The two internal sections ask different local questions, but both belong to the same spatial theme:

- object: movement, path, destination, holding and viewpoint;
- person: position, movement into a position, and posture.

The examples are sufficient for one coherent lesson if the objective stays at **movement and position relative to a reference point** and the two sections remain explicit. The run/slip examples are supporting contrasts, not the centre of the lesson.

**Decision:** retain candidate 04 as one provisional Lesson, with two sections. Do not split object transfer and posture into separate Lessons at this stage. Reconsider only if the full lesson prototype becomes too long or the learner tasks cannot be taught in one progression.



## Teaching-size test 03 — Thinking/knowledge vs learning/teaching; the different meanings of “show”

### Candidate 05 — Thinking, Knowledge, Meaning, Memory and Learning

The current thinking/knowledge/memory set has 11 indexed examples. The separate learning/teaching set contributes three more:

- learn facts → get facts into one's head / get knowledge of them;
- be taught → be taught;
- teach a subject → give training or teaching.

These three fit the same broad knowledge theme:

- thinking/judging: what view or opinion a person has;
- knowing/understanding/meaning: what knowledge or sense a person has;
- remembering/forgetting: what is kept in or put out of the mind;
- learning/teaching: how knowledge is acquired or given.

**Project-created test prompts based on the source examples:**

1. “I think this is so.” Choose a thought/opinion construction, not a knowledge construction.
2. “I know this fact.” Choose “be certain of it” or “have knowledge of it” according to the intended sense.
3. “I understand the argument.” Use “see the point of the argument”.
4. “I remember the fact.” Compare “have a memory of it” and “keep it in mind”.
5. “I learned the facts.” Express the acquisition of knowledge: “get the facts into one's head”.
6. “He taught the subject.” Express the giving of teaching/training.
7. “He was taught.” Preserve the passive relation; do not invent a different Basic rendering merely to avoid repeating the ordinary word.

**Test result:** the learner is still identifying a mental/knowledge relation, but the relation can be a state (know), an operation (think), retention (remember), acquisition (learn) or transfer (teach). These form a coherent knowledge theme with enough indexed material (14 examples) for one lesson with short subsections.

**Decision:** merge learn/teach into candidate 05. Retitle the candidate **Thought, Knowledge & Learning** or **Thinking, Knowledge, Memory & Learning**. Do not keep candidate 07 as a separate learning/teaching Lesson.

### The different meanings of “show” should remain together

The source gives four distinct meanings:

- show the effect of the past on the present → make it clear;
- show pictures → let someone see them / put them on view;
- show old buildings → take someone round;
- show interest → give signs of it.

And one related attempt:

- try to show an interest → make an attempt to give signs of interest.

The important point is that these are **different meanings of the same ordinary English word**. The project rule says to compare those meanings together, not scatter isolated “show = ...” answers into separate lessons.

**Project-created test prompts based on the source examples:**

1. A speaker wants to show how the past affects the present. The intended action is making the relation clear.
2. A person shows pictures. The intended action is making the pictures visible to someone.
3. A guide shows visitors old buildings. The intended action is taking the visitors round.
4. A person shows interest. The intended action is giving signs of an attitude.
5. A person tries to show interest. The expression must retain both the attempt and the signs of interest.

These five prompts expose why “show” has no single Basic replacement. They form a compact contrast set, but not yet a full independent Lesson.

**Decision:** keep all show senses together as a multi-meaning subsection in the Communication & Information theme, with a cross-link from “show interest” to the Feeling/Attitude theme. Keep “try” as part of the final example / the supporting “make an attempt” pattern, not as a standalone Lesson.

### Boundary decision after Teaching-size test 03

- Candidate 05 absorbs learn/teach and remains one provisional Lesson.
- Candidate 07 is removed as an independent Lesson.
- All show meanings stay together in one comparison set under Communication & Information; “show interest” links to Feeling/Attitude but is not duplicated as a separate show lesson.
- “Make an attempt” remains a supporting expression pattern, not a separate course unit.
