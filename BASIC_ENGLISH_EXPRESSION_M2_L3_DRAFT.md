# Module 2 · Lesson 3 — Physical Effects: Damage, Change & Result

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English*, pp.246–247 and p.254. The lesson uses the existing Step 17 foundation for past participles, be + past participle and get + past participle; it does not reteach that full system. Project-created contexts and exercises are labelled as teaching material. Clarity, answer coverage and actual learner time still require review.

## What this lesson teaches

A physical event can be described in different ways:

1. the action that causes a change;
2. the damage or effect produced;
3. the state of the thing after the action;
4. a change that happens by itself, without an agent being named.

This is the central decision in this lesson. It is different from C02, which teaches the bodily action or contact itself, and from C04, which teaches movement and position.

## 1. Cutting and breaking: action, result and state

The source uses a thread, a cake and a plate to show different ways of describing a physical change.

| Situation | Basic expression from the source | What is being described |
|---|---|---|
| cut through a thread or cake | get it cut | The object is caused or arranged to be cut |
| a thread after it has been cut | the thread is broken | The resulting state of the thread |
| have a thread broken | get it broken | The object is caused or arranged to enter the broken state |
| smash a plate | the plate may be smashed | A possible caused action/result |
| a plate breaks by itself | it comes to bits | A change that happens by itself; no agent is introduced |

Keep the differences clear:

- **get it cut** describes having the cutting done to the object;
- **the thread is broken** describes the state of the thread, without saying who broke it;
- **get it broken** describes getting the object into that state;
- **the plate may be smashed** describes a possible result of a caused action;
- **it comes to bits** describes the plate breaking by itself.

The last example is especially important. If the plate breaks by itself, do not invent a person who smashed it. The source uses **come to bits** for this self-occurring change.

## 2. Destroy and hurt are not the same event

The source later gives two related but different ideas:

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expressions from the source | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| destroy an object | burn it / smash it / break it | The thing is destroyed by a concrete physical action |
| destroy something more generally | put an end to it | The thing or process is brought to an end |
| destroy an animal | put an end to it | The animal's life is ended |
| hurt someone | give them pain / give wounds / do serious damage | The person suffers pain, wounds or injury |
| attempt to destroy something but fail | do no more than hurt it | The result is injury, not destruction |

A few distinctions matter:

- **destroy** does not have one fixed Basic replacement. The source describes what actually happens: burning, smashing, breaking or putting an end to something.
- **hurt** describes pain, wounds or damage. It is not simply another word for **hit**.
- **hit / give a blow** belongs to C02 when the focus is forceful contact. **Hurt / give pain / give wounds / do serious damage** belongs here when the focus is the injury or effect.
- The source says that some attempts at destroying an animal do no more than hurt it. That contrast helps show that causing pain or wounds is not the same as destroying.

## 3. A short reminder from Step 17

The current App's Step 17, sections 4–6, already teaches:

- past participle forms;
- **be + past participle** for a passive or result description;
- **get + past participle** for getting something done or bringing it into a result state.

C03 uses those structures in its own physical examples. It does not teach the whole rule again.

| Form | C03 example | Use in this lesson |
|---|---|---|
| be + past participle | the thread is broken | The thread is in the resulting state |
| get + past participle | get it cut / get it broken | The object is caused or arranged to undergo the action |
| modal + be + past participle | the plate may be smashed | A possible caused result |
| ordinary Basic expression | it comes to bits | A change that happens by itself |

If you are unsure how the past participle works, return to **Systematic Learning → Step 17 → sections 4–6** before doing the result-form practice.

Do not confuse a past participle used in a sentence with simply adding **-ed** to make a new word. The source uses **broken**, **smashed** and **cut**; these forms do not all follow the same spelling pattern.

## 4. How to choose the expression

When you describe a physical effect, ask:

1. **What action causes the change?** Is something cut, broken, smashed or destroyed?
2. **What happens to the object?** Is it cut, broken, smashed or brought to an end?
3. **Are you describing the action or the resulting state?** Compare cutting a thread with saying that the thread is broken.
4. **Is an agent stated?** If the plate breaks by itself, use the source's self-occurring expression; do not invent an agent.
5. **Is the person hurt, or is the object destroyed?** Pain/wounds/damage are not automatically destruction.
6. **Is the sentence actually about contact or movement?** A blow as contact belongs to C02; throwing or moving something belongs to C04.

The correct expression depends on the event and result, not only on the ordinary-English verb.

## Practice

### A. Identify the action, result or state

Choose the best description.

1. A person cuts through a thread and gets it cut.
   - A. The object is caused or arranged to be cut
   - B. The thread breaks by itself
   - C. The object is moved across a place

2. The thread is broken.
   - A. The thread is being moved
   - B. The thread is in a resulting state
   - C. Someone is touching the thread by chance

3. Someone gets the thread broken.
   - A. The thread is caused or arranged to enter the broken state
   - B. The thread is simply held in the hand
   - C. The thread is put out of the mouth

4. A plate may be smashed.
   - A. A possible caused action/result is described
   - B. The plate moves by itself to another place
   - C. Someone gives the plate a touch

5. A plate breaks by itself and comes to bits.
   - A. A person smashes it
   - B. The plate changes by itself; no agent is named
   - C. Someone gets the plate cut

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — Get it cut describes having the cutting done to the object.
2. B — Be + past participle describes the resulting state.
3. A — Get + past participle describes getting the object into the broken state.
4. A — The source uses may be smashed for a possible caused result.
5. B — The source uses come to bits when the plate breaks by itself.

### B. Distinguish destroy from hurt

Choose the expression that preserves the result.

1. An object is destroyed by burning it.
   - A. burn it
   - B. give it a rub
   - C. come up against it

2. Something is destroyed by bringing it to an end.
   - A. put an end to it
   - B. put a hand on it
   - C. give it a touch

3. A person is hurt and feels pain.
   - A. give them pain
   - B. give them a song
   - C. have a meal

4. A person is hurt and has wounds.
   - A. give wounds
   - B. put it out of the mouth
   - C. make a statement

5. An attempt to destroy an animal does not destroy it but causes pain and wounds.
   - A. it is only hurt
   - B. it comes to bits
   - C. it is sent across the river

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source gives burning as one concrete way of destroying an object.
2. A — Put an end to it expresses bringing it to an end.
3. A — The source uses give them pain for hurt.
4. A — The source includes giving wounds as a way of describing hurt.
5. A — The source explicitly contrasts an unsuccessful attempt to destroy with the lesser result of hurting.

### C. Keep the neighbouring lessons separate

1. Someone gives another person a blow. What is the focus: contact or injury?

   **Answer:** If the focus is the physical impact, it belongs to C02. If the focus is the pain or wound caused, it belongs to C03.

2. A plate breaks by itself and comes to bits. Does this belong to C02?

   **Answer:** No. The main point is the change and result, so it belongs to C03.

3. Someone throws a ball over a wall. Does this belong to C03?

   **Answer:** No. The main point is movement and path, so it belongs to C04.

### D. Reverse understanding

Explain what each Basic expression says about the physical event:

1. get it cut;
2. the thread is broken;
3. get it broken;
4. the plate may be smashed;
5. it comes to bits;
6. burn it / smash it / break it;
7. put an end to it;
8. give them pain;
9. give wounds;
10. do serious damage.

**Suggested answers**

1. Have the object cut.
2. The thread is in the broken state.
3. Get the object into the broken state.
4. The plate may be smashed by a caused action.
5. The plate breaks by itself into pieces.
6. Destroy the object through a concrete physical action.
7. Bring the thing or life/process to an end.
8. Cause someone to feel pain.
9. Cause wounds.
10. Cause serious injury or damage.

### E. Step 17 prerequisite check

Identify the structure in each expression:

1. the thread is broken;
2. get it broken;
3. the plate may be smashed.

**Answers**

1. be + past participle;
2. get + past participle;
3. may + be + past participle.

If these forms are not clear, review Step 17, sections 4–6. This lesson only applies the existing foundation to physical effects.

### F. Independent construction

Choose one situation:

- an object is cut or broken by someone;
- a plate breaks by itself;
- something is destroyed;
- a person is hurt but not destroyed.

Write the actual event first. Then choose a Basic expression that preserves whether the sentence describes the action, the effect, the resulting state or a self-occurring change. Explain why a nearby expression would change the meaning.
