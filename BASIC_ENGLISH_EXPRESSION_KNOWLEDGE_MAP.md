# Basic English Expression System — Current Knowledge Map

> 状态：**研究中间成果，不是最终课程目录。**
>
> 本文用于会话切换和后续课程设计的共同基线。它只记录目前已经核实、已经归类和仍未解决的内容；不把候选 Lesson、项目归类或推测写成原作者的正式分类。

## 1. 当前任务到底是什么

当前不是决定“总共几个 Lesson”，也不是把原书章节机械切成课程。

当前任务是先回答：

> **Basic English 现有资料到底构成了哪些不同层次的能力？这些层次之间有什么关系、先修关系和重叠？**

只有这个关系图稳定后，才能从学习任务的实际变化决定 Module / Lesson 边界。

当前设计原则：

- 不按页码、词条数量、原书章节数量或旧 Lesson 编号拆课。
- 不为了凑一个预定 Lesson 数量而制造课程。
- 同一种判断任务和表达方法自然连续，就放在一起。
- 学习者需要进行新的判断，或需要掌握明显不同的表达方法时，才考虑拆分。
- 基础工具已经由 Teaching / 45 Steps 承担的内容，表达课程调用它们，不重复造课。
- Meaning → Basic 是贯穿课程的训练方法，不是与原书章节并列的独立知识模块。

## 2. 已确认的基础层

### 2.1 850词

项目资料确认 850 词分为：

- 100 Operations
- 600 Things
  - 400 general nouns
  - 200 picturable objects
- 150 Qualities
  - 100 adjectives
  - 50 adjectival opposites

这里的 **100 Operations 不是100个普通动词**。它包含核心 Operators 以及其他结构词。

### 2.2 16个核心 Operators

当前项目统一采用：

**be, come, do, get, have, give, go, keep, let, make, put, say, see, seem, send, take**

项目资料还明确区分：

- may、will 等辅助词不计入这16个核心 Operators。
- Operators 不能只按中文词义学习；需要理解基本动作图景、词形变化和搭配关系。
- 例如 come / go 涉及参照位置；give / get 与 to / from 有明确的关系方向。

### 2.3 Directives

项目资料区分：

**20个 proper Directives：**

at, with, from, against, to, across, after, among, before, about, through, down, between, up, under, on, over, off, by, in

另外：

- out 是具有方向意义的副词，不属于20个 proper Directives。
- for、of、till 是非方向性介词。
- into、upon、down from 等可以由多个方向单位组合形成。
- 《The ABC of Basic English》的 Names of Directions 章节有21个词条，但这与“20个 proper Directives”的分类口径不同。

因此，**16 × 20 不是320条现成搭配规则**。原作者的组合思想是：根据 Operator、Directive、对象和实际关系判断组合是否成立，再学习其抽象扩展和固定用法。

## 3. 已确认的主要内容层

目前可以把原书材料理解为几个不同的问题层，而不是一个平面词表。

### A. 基础表达工具

包括：

- Operators
- Directives
- 基本句子结构
- 代词、时态、比较等基础规则
- Other Word-Forms：-er、-ing、-ed 等

这些属于“**怎样使用 Basic 的基础零件**”。

当前原则：已有 Teaching / 45 Steps 承担的基础规则不在 Expression System 中重复造一套课程。

### B. Expansions：词义为什么可以扩大或收窄

《The ABC of Basic English》中的 Names of Things and Qualities 解决的是：

> **同一个 Basic 词，为什么可以在不同对象或语境中承担相关但不同的意义？**

目前已经核对的主要类型：

#### Names of Things

- 身体部位 → 相似的事物部位
- 感官性质上的相似
- 用途或行为上的相似
- “as if” 比较和非实体对象

#### Names of Qualities

- 根据人的反应形成的性质延伸
- 一种性质推到经常伴随的另一种性质

#### Special use

- 材料名称 → 由该材料形成的具体物件
- 类别 → 其中重要的特定子类
- 某个语境中的默认特定意义

另外，-er / -ing / -ed 与意义扩展存在交叉，但词尾本身仍属于基础词形系统，不能把两者混成一套规则。

**当前课程意义：**

这不是“如何把普通英语动词改写成 Basic”的同一个问题，而是“如何理解 Basic 词在不同语境中的意义”。

### C. Names of Acts：理解 Operators 本身

原书 pp.162–186 的 Names of Acts 与后面的 Some Common Needs 必须分开。

当前项目把 Names of Acts 理解为：

> **理解核心 Operators 自身怎样工作、怎样承担不同动作意义。**

它是表达工具层，不等同于日常动作的应用题库。

### D. Names of Directions：理解方向/关系工具

这一层解决：

> **一个 Operator 与方向/关系组合时，空间关系是什么？这个关系怎样扩展到时间、目的、原因、思想、控制等抽象关系？**

当前已建立：

- Directives 索引
- Operator × Directive 交叉研究
- 原书实例与当前 App 练习的重新分类

重要结论：

当前 App 原来的“Operator + Direction”练习其实混合了多种结构，例如：

- Operator + Directive
- Operator + Quality
- Operator + Name + Preposition
- Name of Act + Directive
- 特殊方向副词构式

因此不能把它们继续当作一个单一语法规则。

### E. 250 Fixed Word-Groups

这是另一个独立层：

> **有些 Basic 组合不能只靠单词字面意义自然推导，必须作为固定用法掌握。**

目前已核对原书的250项结构：

- 10个 Operators × 每组5例 = 50
- 8个关系词 × 每组10例 = 80
- 10个其他 Operations / Directions 词项的特殊用法 = 20
- Basic 词表其他类别的特殊用法 = 100

合计 **250**。

100项部分目前的分类记录为：

- Operations：13
- General Names：51
- Picturable Things：18
- Names of Qualities：15
- Opposites：3

同时必须区分两份“50项”：

1. 250项内部的第一组50项；
2. A Basic Selection pp.264–269 另外列出的50个 **The Most Necessary Fixed Word-Groups**。

它们不是同一份清单。

当前课程原则：

- 50项最必要固定词组应优先进入相关主题的例句、练习和复习。
- 250项其余部分主要作为扩展查询/遇到时学习。
- 不把250项或50项机械做成250课/50课。

### F. Some Common Needs：真正的实际表达应用

这是目前与“Expression System”最直接相关的一层。

原书 A Basic Selection 的第一部分：

**Some Common Needs，pp.242–262**

包括：

- Simple Acts
- Complex Acts
- Other Suggestions

它解决的问题是：

> **普通英语中经常需要表达、但850词表没有直接提供一个普通动词时，怎样利用 Basic 的有限工具把意思重新组织出来。**

这是目前课程设计的**主要应用材料**。

## 4. Some Common Needs 已完成的第一轮意义归类

以下是项目为了教学而做的内部归类，**不是原书新增的章节分类**。

### Simple Acts

#### 沟通与信息

speak、tell、sing、hear、listen、write、read、send

这一组已经有候选稿。

其中 speak 的三个原书语境尤其重要：

- 对某人说话 → say something to him
- 在会议上讲话 → give a talk / make a statement
- 会说一种语言 → have a knowledge of it

这已经证明：

> **不能建立“普通英语词 = 一个 Basic 替换答案”的课程模型。**

必须先判断语境中的实际意思。

#### 饮食与身体动作

eat、bite、spit、touch、hit、scratch、cut、break

#### 移动与身体姿势

throw、catch、hold、bring、lie、stand、sit、run、slip

### Complex Acts

#### 思考、知识与记忆

think、know、understand、mean、remember、forget

#### 感受、喜好、需要与学习

feel、like、want、need、learn、teach、show、try

#### 社会活动与生活过程

buy、sell、promise、find、lose、destroy、hurt、grow、sleep、die、begin、finish

这里的“社会活动与生活过程”是项目为了理解材料做的内部归类；原书仍然使用 Simple Acts / Complex Acts / Other Suggestions 这三个一级分组。

### Other Suggestions

目前第一轮归类为：

#### 能力、义务与意愿

can、ought、shall

#### 数量与时间

each、altogether、whole、few、many、too、once、ago、already、yet、since、next、always

#### 条件、性质与常见名称

unless、busy、deaf、world、life、hill、home、address

## 5. A Basic Selection 不能只看 Some Common Needs

原书的 A Basic Selection 还有另外两部分：

### Words Formed by Endings

pp.262–264

它提供实际需要的词尾派生形式。

当前原则：

- 与具体表达一起使用。
- 遇到实际词形问题时解释。
- 不重复建立一套与基础词形规则完全相同的课程。

### The Most Necessary Fixed Word-Groups

pp.264–269

这是从固定词组中另外挑出的50项最难缺少的内容。

当前原则：

- 作为跨主题的优先材料。
- 放进相关主题例句、练习和复习。
- 不按50项逐个开课。

## 6. Meaning → Basic 是方法，不是内容类别

项目已经建立 Meaning → Basic 练习原型。

其真正任务是：

**普通英语意思 → 判断当前语境 → 选择/组织 Basic 表达 → 反向检查意思是否保留**

当前第一组已验证的例子包括：

- insert → put
- remove → take
- transfer → give
- construct → make
- approach → come / go
- extinguish → make + go

这里已经出现一个关键设计结论：

同一个普通英语词可能有多个正确 Basic 表达；答案必须根据：

- source / destination
- 操作方向
- 对象
- 结果
- 语境

来判断。

因此 Meaning → Basic 不能设计成简单的一对一词汇替换。

## 7. 当前已经存在的两个教学原型

### 原型 A：speak

目标不是教“speak = 某个 Basic 词”。

而是让学习者经历：

1. 看三个真实语境。
2. 判断三个 speak 实际表达的意义不同。
3. 比较 Basic 结构。
4. 自己构造。
5. 反向理解。
6. 分别检查词汇、语法和语义。

**它目前的价值是验证“语境决定表达”的教学方法。**

它暂时不能证明“speak 必须独占一个正式 Lesson”。

### 原型 B：Names of Things and Qualities

当前身体部位扩展原型以：

head、foot、leg、mouth、neck 等为材料，

让学习者理解：

> 根本意义 → 扩展意义 → 两者之间为什么存在联系 → 反向理解

它验证的是另一种完全不同的学习任务：

> **理解一个 Basic 词为什么能够承担多个相关意义。**

因此，它与 speak 原型已经显示出明显不同的认知任务。

## 8. 当前知识之间的关系

目前可以稳定确认的关系是：

**850词**
↓
提供最基本的词汇材料

**Operators / Directives / 词形 / 句子结构**
↓
提供使用这些词汇的基础工具

**Names of Acts / Names of Directions**
↓
解释这些基础工具本身的意义和扩展方式

**Names of Things and Qualities**
↓
解释同一个词如何通过意义联系承担不同对象/性质

**250 Fixed Word-Groups**
↓
处理不能仅凭字面意义自由推出的固定用法

**Some Common Needs**
↓
把上述工具真正用于“普通英语意思 → Basic表达”

**Words Formed by Endings / 50项最必要固定词组**
↓
作为应用过程中的补充和高频材料

**Meaning → Basic**
↓
贯穿上述实际表达学习，负责训练“从意思出发表达”，而不是单独成为一个知识模块

这张关系图是目前最重要的课程设计基础。

## 14. Theme-boundary audit 01 — Simple Acts: eating / body action / physical contact / change of form

### 14.1 Source material checked

The relevant `Some Common Needs / Simple Acts` material has been checked at the level of the original examples and the project index.

The source sequence first treats:

- eat
- bite
- spit

and then moves through:

- touch
- hit
- scratch
- cut
- break

The source itself makes an important internal transition: after the touch/hit/scratch/cut/break examples, it explicitly identifies **cutting and breaking as the important operations connected with changes of form**, and then moves on to changing the places of things. This is direct evidence that “physical action” is not one undifferentiated learning problem.

### 14.2 First candidate learning tasks

#### Task A — Taking in / biting / putting out

Examples:

- eat → take food / have a meal
- bite → have a bite of it / at it
- spit → put it out of your mouth
- eat until unable to continue → be unable to get through it

The common learning problem is not “food vocabulary”.

It is:

> **How is a bodily action represented when the ordinary verb is replaced by an Operator plus a Name and/or a relation describing the object or route?**

The learner must notice:

- what is being acted on;
- whether the action is intake, biting, or expulsion;
- whether a body part or body location must be stated;
- whether a direction such as out / of is needed;
- whether the expression describes the action itself or completion/result.

This is a coherent candidate task, but it is not yet proven to deserve a complete Lesson by itself.

#### Task B — Contact and force

Examples:

- touch → put a hand/finger/body part on it / give it a touch
- touch by chance → come up against it
- hit → give it a blow
- scratch → make a mark on it / give it a rub / get one's nails into it

The central judgment changes:

> **What kind of physical contact is occurring, and what relation or effect must Basic make explicit?**

The same ordinary verb family changes its Basic construction when the physical relation changes:

- simple contact;
- accidental contact;
- forceful contact;
- contact producing a mark;
- contact involving a body part.

This is a distinct learning task from simply expressing eating/biting/spitting.

#### Task C — Change of form / result

Examples:

- cut → give it a cut / get it cut
- break → be broken / get it broken
- smash → may be smashed
- break by itself → come to bits

The central judgment changes again:

> **Is the sentence describing an action that changes an object's form, the resulting state, or the object's coming apart by itself?**

This introduces:

- active action vs resulting state;
- get + object + past participle;
- be + past participle;
- self-occurring change;
- the result of cutting/breaking.

The original source explicitly identifies cutting and breaking as the important operations for changes of form, so this boundary is supported by the source itself rather than being an arbitrary project grouping.

### 14.3 Current boundary conclusion

**Confirmed design evidence:**

The current project label “饮食与身体动作” is too broad to be treated as a single established Lesson merely because the examples are adjacent in the source.

There are at least **three different candidate learning tasks**:

1. bodily intake / biting / expulsion;
2. contact / force / physical effect;
3. change of form / result.

However, this does **not** yet mean “three Lessons”.

The next design test must ask whether Task A is large and coherent enough to justify its own teaching unit, and whether Tasks B and C are better taught together as one physical-action progression or separately.

### 14.4 What this changes in the course map

The previous first-round grouping:

> Simple Acts → “饮食与身体动作”

should now be treated only as a **source-material bucket**, not a candidate Lesson.

For curriculum design purposes it should temporarily be represented as:

> **Simple Acts / physical actions**
> → bodily intake and expulsion
> → contact and force
> → change of form

This is a better representation of the actual learning-task boundaries.

- Whether the next “movement / posture” material naturally continues from these tasks or creates a new decision problem.
- Whether any of the fixed word-groups should be introduced inside these tasks or left for later reinforcement.

No formal Lesson number is assigned from this audit.

## 16. Theme-boundary audit 02 — Simple Acts: movement, transfer and body posture

### 16.1 Source material checked

The next Simple Acts group contains:

- throw
- catch
- hold
- bring
- lie
- stand
- sit
- run
- slip

and also gives concrete combinations such as:

- take your arm out of your coat
- put money into your pocket
- go quickly out of the house

### 16.2 Candidate learning task A — Moving / transferring an object

Examples include:

- throw a ball → send the ball to a baby
- throw over/across → send the ball over the wall / across the river
- catch → get it in the hand
- hold → get a grip of it / keep it / have it in the hand
- bring delicate things → take them / come with them

The main decision problem is:

> **What happens to the object, where is it going, and whose point of view determines the Basic expression?**

This involves:

- object movement;
- destination or path;
- source/recipient;
- holding vs transferring;
- bring/take and come/go viewpoint.

This is closely related to the already established Operator + Directive / source-destination framework, so the Expression System should **use that existing tool rather than create a new abstract “movement grammar” lesson**.

### 16.3 Candidate learning task B — Body position and change of posture

Examples include:

- lie → be on it / rest on it
- stand up → get up
- sit → take a seat / be seated

The central problem changes:

> **Is the person describing a position/state, or the action that changes the body into that position?**

This introduces a compact but distinct contrast:

- state: be on it / be seated;
- change into position: get up / take a seat.

This is not the same decision problem as moving an object from one place to another.

### 16.4 Candidate learning task C — Running and slipping

Examples include:

- run quickly → go quickly
- run for health → take a run
- run away from danger → go (off) at a run
- slip → have a slip / have a fall

These examples reinforce an important principle already seen in `speak`:

> the ordinary English word does not determine one fixed Basic expression; the surrounding meaning does.

For run, the relevant meaning may be:

- movement speed;
- an activity undertaken for a purpose;
- escape/direction.

For slip, the Basic expression focuses on the event/result as a noun.

This makes run a useful cross-topic example rather than automatically a complete Lesson.

### 16.5 Current boundary conclusion

The previous bucket “移动与身体姿势” is also too broad to be treated as one established Lesson.

The material currently separates into:

1. **object movement / transfer / viewpoint**;
2. **body position and posture change**;
3. **movement-purpose / direction / accidental event**, with run and slip acting partly as cross-topic examples.

The first task heavily reuses existing Operator + Directive and source/destination knowledge. It therefore should not automatically become a new “movement grammar” lesson.

The second task is a genuinely different semantic decision problem: **state vs change of state**.

The third task is better treated as a set of meaning contrasts until more material shows that it forms a sufficiently coherent learning unit.

### 16.6 Relationship to Theme Audit 01

There is now a clearer boundary between the first two Simple Acts buckets:

- **physical contact / force / change of form** asks what physical effect an action produces;
- **movement / transfer / posture** asks where an object/person is, moves, or changes position.

They share Operators and Directives, but the learner's semantic decision is different.

Therefore the shared grammar/tool does **not** imply that they should be one Lesson.

No formal Lesson number is assigned.

## 18. Theme-boundary audit 03 — Complex Acts: thinking, knowledge and memory

### 18.1 Source material checked

The first Complex Acts group contains:

- think
- know
- understand
- mean
- remember
- forget

The source examples use several Basic construction types:

- give thought to
- be in thought
- be of the opinion
- take the view that
- be certain of
- be conscious of
- have knowledge / experience of
- be clear about
- have a grip of
- see the point of view
- have the sense of
- have a memory of
- keep in mind
- put out of the mind
- have no memory of

### 18.2 The common learning problem

Unlike the physical-action groups, these examples are not mainly about a visible physical relation.

The common problem is:

> **A normal English mental verb compresses a mental state, relation or operation into one word; Basic often makes that hidden relation explicit with a Name, Quality, Operator and/or Directive.**

Examples:

- think → give thought to
- know → have knowledge of / be certain of
- understand → be clear about / have a grip of / see the point of
- mean → have the sense of
- remember → have a memory of / keep in mind
- forget → put it out of the mind / have no memory of it

This is a substantially more coherent semantic task than the first two Simple Acts buckets.

### 18.3 Internal differences

There is still an internal contrast:

#### A. Thinking / judging / knowing / understanding / meaning

The learner has to distinguish:

- an active mental process: think;
- an opinion: be of the opinion;
- certainty/knowledge: be certain of / have knowledge of;
- clarity or grasp: be clear about / have a grip of;
- interpretation/meaning: have the sense of.

These are not interchangeable, even though they are all “mental”.

#### B. Remembering / forgetting

The learner must express:

- possession of a memory;
- keeping something in mind;
- removing something from the mind;
- absence of memory.

The Basic expressions therefore make memory direction/state visible:

- have a memory of;
- keep it in mind;
- put it out of the mind;
- have no memory of it.

### 18.4 Current boundary conclusion

The two internal parts are related closely enough that **there is currently stronger evidence for treating them as one coherent teaching theme than for splitting them immediately**.

The key common task is:

> **Identify the mental relation first, then choose the Basic structure that makes that relation explicit.**

Remember/forget can be used as the later part of the same progression because they introduce a particularly clear contrast between keeping something in mind and putting it out of the mind.

This is an important calibration result:

- the source's Complex Acts / thinking-knowledge-memory grouping has a coherent semantic center;
- unlike the earlier “eating/body action” bucket, it does not currently show a strong enough task boundary to justify an automatic split.

This still does **not** assign a final Lesson number.

### 18.5 Relationship to previous audits

The comparison now shows three different situations:

1. **Communication:** strong common method, but the complete scope still needs testing.
2. **Physical action:** source-adjacent examples contain several different decision problems.
3. **Thinking / knowledge / memory:** the whole first Complex Acts group currently has a relatively coherent decision problem.

Therefore the curriculum cannot be designed simply by giving every source subsection one Lesson. The actual semantic task must decide.
