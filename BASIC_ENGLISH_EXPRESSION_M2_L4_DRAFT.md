# Module 2 · Lesson 4 — Movement & Position: Transfer, Posture & Viewpoint

> **Draft status:** first complete teaching-content draft. It is based on Ogden's *The ABC of Basic English*, especially pp.248 and 250. The source examples are distinguished from project-created contexts and exercises. Existing Operators and Directives are prerequisites; this lesson applies them and does not reteach the foundation. Clarity, answer coverage and actual learner time still require review.

## What this lesson teaches

When something or someone moves, ask:

- what is moving;
- where it starts and where it goes;
- what path it takes;
- whose viewpoint is being used;
- whether the sentence describes movement or a position.

This lesson has two core parts:

1. moving, sending, catching, holding and carrying objects;
2. describing body position and a change of posture.

The source also uses **run** and **slip** in more than one sense. These are kept as short meaning contrasts, not turned into separate lessons.

## 1. Moving, sending, catching and holding an object

The source begins with moving a thing from one place to another.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What the expression makes clear |
|---|---|---|
| throw a ball to a baby | send the ball to a baby | The receiver or destination |
| throw a ball over a wall | send the ball over the wall | The path over an obstacle |
| throw a ball across a river | send the ball across the river | The path from one side to the other |
| catch a ball | get it in the hand | The object arrives in the hand |
| hold a ball | get a grip of it / keep it / have it in the hand | The object is held or kept |
| be given something to hold | take it | The person receives and takes the object |
| bring or take delicate things such as eggs | take them / come with them | The object is carried rather than thrown |

A few distinctions matter:

- **send** describes the movement towards a receiver or along a path. The path words **over** and **across** are important; do not remove them.
- **get it in the hand** describes the result of catching: the ball is now in the hand.
- **get a grip of it**, **keep it** and **have it in the hand** describe holding from related viewpoints. They are not three unrelated actions.
- When something is simply given to you to hold, the source says **take it**. When you already have hold of it, you **have a grip of it**.
- For delicate things such as eggs, the source says it is safer to bring or take them than to throw them; it also uses **come with them**. The choice depends on the movement and reference point in the context. Do not memorize bring = one fixed Basic word or take = one fixed Basic word.

The foundation lesson on Directives already teaches the general direction words. Here, use them to describe the actual path or destination; do not repeat the whole Directives lesson.

## 2. Body position and change of posture

The source next describes the position of a person or thing.

| Ordinary English meaning | Basic expression from the source | What is being described |
|---|---|---|
| lie on something | be on it / be resting on it | A position or state |
| get upright after lying down | stand / get up | A change into an upright position |
| remain standing | be on one's feet / still be standing | A continuing position |
| something stands in a place | be placed in a certain position | The position of an object |
| sit | take a seat / be seated | Moving into or being in a sitting position |
| make a move | make a move in some direction | A change of position or direction |

Keep a position separate from the action that changes it:

- **be on it / be resting on it** describes where the person or thing is;
- **get up** describes the change from lying down to an upright position;
- **be on one's feet** describes the state after getting up;
- **take a seat** describes the action of sitting down;
- **be seated** describes the resulting sitting position.

The same position word can also describe an object: the source says that things which stand are placed in a certain position. Do not assume that every use of stand describes a person getting up.

## 3. Run and slip: short meaning contrasts

The source shows that one ordinary word can describe different events.

### run

| Context | Basic expression from the source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| move quickly because time is short | go quickly | Speed of movement |
| run for health or exercise | take a run | An activity undertaken for health |
| run away from danger | go (off) at a run | Moving away to escape danger |

Do not treat **run** as one fixed replacement. First identify whether the sentence describes speed, exercise or escape.

### slip

The source gives two uses:

| Context | Basic expression from the source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| lose one's footing on a little ice | have a slip / have a fall | An accidental loss of footing |
| a quick, simple action | take your arm out of your coat; put money into your pocket; go quickly out of the house | A small movement done quickly and simply |

The second use is a source-listed extension of **slip**. It does not mean that every quick action must be described this way.

## 4. Two supporting examples about balance and movement

The source also says:

- a sudden noise may **throw** or **put us off our balance**;
- a blow, given hard enough, will **send us violently against the table**.

These examples show two different effects:

- losing balance;
- being moved against another object.

The physical blow itself is a C02 contact/impact example. C04 is concerned with the resulting balance or movement. Keep this as a short comparison rather than a new section of movement rules.

## 5. How to choose the expression

When you meet a movement or position verb, ask:

1. **What is moving?** A ball, another object, a person or a body part?
2. **Where does it go?** Is there a receiver, destination or path?
3. **What is the reference point?** Is the movement described from the speaker's position or from another place?
4. **Is the sentence about movement or position?** Compare getting up with being on one's feet.
5. **Is it a special context for run or slip?** Identify speed, exercise, escape, loss of footing or a quick simple action.
6. **Is the main event actually contact or damage?** A blow as contact belongs to C02; the injury/effect belongs to C03.

The same object can be described from different viewpoints, and the same ordinary word can refer to different events. Identify the movement or position before choosing the Basic expression.

## Practice

### A. Identify the movement and path

Choose the expression that matches the event.

1. A person throws a ball to a baby.
   - A. send the ball to the baby
   - B. take a seat
   - C. have a slip

2. A person throws a ball across a river.
   - A. get it in the hand
   - B. send the ball across the river
   - C. be on one's feet

3. Someone catches a ball.
   - A. get it in the hand
   - B. put it out of the mouth
   - C. make a mark on it

4. Someone is holding a ball and has a firm hold of it.
   - A. have a grip of it
   - B. go quickly
   - C. come to bits

5. Someone is given an object to hold.
   - A. take it
   - B. throw it across the river
   - C. have a fall

6. Someone carries delicate eggs rather than throwing them.
   - A. take them / come with them
   - B. give them a blow
   - C. get them broken

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — The source uses send for throwing an object to a receiver.
2. B — Across states the path.
3. A — Catching is described by getting the object in the hand.
4. A — The source uses get a grip of it for having a firm hold.
5. A — When given something to hold, the source says take it.
6. A — The source says delicate things are safer to take or come with than to throw.

### B. Distinguish position from posture change

Choose the expression that matches the event.

1. A person is lying on a bed.
   - A. be on it / be resting on it
   - B. get up
   - C. go off at a run

2. A person moves from lying down to an upright position.
   - A. get up
   - B. have a slip
   - C. put money into a pocket

3. A person is still standing after getting up.
   - A. be on one's feet
   - B. take a seat
   - C. get a grip of it

4. A tired person sits down.
   - A. take a seat
   - B. send it across the river
   - C. make a mark on it

5. A thing is standing in a certain place.
   - A. be placed in a certain position
   - B. go quickly
   - C. give it a rub

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — This describes the position, not a change of posture.
2. A — Get up describes the change into an upright position.
3. A — Being on one's feet describes the continuing position.
4. A — Take a seat describes the action of sitting down.
5. A — The source also uses stand for an object's placed position.

### C. Distinguish the meanings of run and slip

Choose the Basic expression.

1. Someone runs because time is short.
   - A. go quickly
   - B. take a run
   - C. have a fall

2. Someone runs to keep healthy.
   - A. go off at a run
   - B. take a run
   - C. get it in the hand

3. Someone runs away from danger.
   - A. go (off) at a run
   - B. be seated
   - C. send it over the wall

4. Someone loses their footing on ice.
   - A. have a slip / have a fall
   - B. take a seat
   - C. come up against it

5. Someone puts money into a pocket as a quick, simple action.
   - A. put money into your pocket
   - B. take a run
   - C. give it a blow

**Answers and reasons**

1. A — This is speed of movement.
2. B — This is an activity undertaken for health.
3. A — This is escape from danger.
4. A — The source describes losing one's footing as having a slip or fall.
5. A — This is one of the source's quick, simple actions associated with slip.

### D. Understand balance and movement

1. A sudden noise throws or puts us off our balance. Is the main point the sound, or the person's change of balance?

   **Answer:** The person's change of balance.

2. A blow, given hard enough, sends us violently against the table. Which part belongs to C02, and which part belongs to C04?

   **Answer:** The blow/contact belongs to C02; the resulting movement against the table belongs to C04.

### E. Reverse understanding

Explain the movement or position described by each Basic expression:

1. send the ball across the river;
2. get it in the hand;
3. have a grip of it;
4. take it;
5. come with them;
6. be resting on it;
7. get up;
8. be on one's feet;
9. take a seat;
10. go quickly;
11. take a run;
12. go off at a run;
13. have a slip / have a fall;
14. take your arm out of your coat;
15. put money into your pocket;
16. go quickly out of the house.

**Suggested answers**

1. Throw the ball from one side to the other.
2. Catch it so that it is in the hand.
3. Hold it firmly.
4. Take the object that has been given to you.
5. Move along with the object or person.
6. Be in a resting position on it.
7. Move from lying down into an upright position.
8. Remain standing.
9. Sit down.
10. Run because speed is needed.
11. Run as an activity for health.
12. Run away from danger.
13. Lose one's footing or fall.
14. Move the arm out of the coat.
15. Move the money into the pocket.
16. Leave the house quickly.

### F. Independent construction

Choose one situation from your own experience:

- moving an object to someone or along a path;
- catching or holding an object;
- carrying something delicate;
- changing from one body position to another;
- moving quickly, exercising or escaping;
- slipping or making a quick simple movement.

Describe the event first. Then choose the Basic expression that preserves the path, destination, viewpoint or position. Explain why a nearby expression would change the meaning.

## Lesson review

You should now be able to:

- express an object's movement, receiver, path and destination;
- distinguish catching, holding and taking;
- choose between take / come with according to the movement and reference point in context;
- distinguish a position from a change of posture;
- distinguish the source's different uses of run and slip;
- recognize when the example is about movement/position rather than contact or physical damage.
